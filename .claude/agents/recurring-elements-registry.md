# Recurring-Elements Registry

The project's single source of truth for **consistency across entries**. At ~1,000
units, the same characters, places, and motifs recur constantly; this file keeps
their descriptions, name-forms, and framing identical everywhere. **Maintained by
the Managing Editor.** A living document — add a row the first time an element
appears, and reuse the canonical wording thereafter.

*Seeded from the primeval history (Gen 1–11), the production starting point. Expand book by book.*
 
---

## 1 · Project-wide conventions (apply to every entry)

- **The divine name.** The Hebrew is **YHWH**. In the **KJV layer**, leave it as
  printed ("the LORD"). In the **faithful translation** and **retelling**, use
  "the LORD" (or "God" where the Hebrew has *Elohim*). In **Behind-the-Text**, you
  may write **YHWH**. Explain the name once, where it first matters.
- **Jewish-tradition sensitivity.** In the Jewish-interpretation strand, respect
  that observant usage avoids writing/pronouncing the name — use "the LORD,"
  "Hashem," or "YHWH" as the context calls for; never "Jehovah" or "Yahweh" inside
  that strand.
- **Islamic-tradition conventions** *(owned by the Comparative-Religion / Islam
  Scholar #13; apply in the Islamic strand and the cross-tradition field).*
  *Allah* is the Arabic word for God — the same God, not a separate deity; write
  "God" in English prose. Give prophets' **Arabic names with an English gloss** on
  first use, then as the strand calls for: *Nuh* (Noah), *Ibrahim* (Abraham),
  *Ismail* (Ishmael), *Ishaq* (Isaac), *Yusuf* (Joseph), *Musa* (Moses), *Dawud*
  (David), *Sulayman* (Solomon), *Yunus* (Jonah), *Maryam* (Mary), *Isa* (Jesus).
  Cite the Qur'an by sura name + number:verse (e.g. *Surah Hud*, 11:25–49). Note —
  don't insert — the Muslim practice of adding "peace be upon him" after a
  prophet's name. Never frame the Qur'an's version as "the Bible story, changed";
  grade any borrowing claim and report Islam's self-understanding as data.
- **Name rendering.** Familiar English forms in the reading layers (Cain, Noah,
  Moses, Mary); transliteration (*Qayin*, *Hevel*) only in Behind-the-Text;
  Arabic forms (*Nuh*, *Ibrahim*) in the Islamic strand and cross-tradition field.
- **Explaining references.** A short parenthetical aside in the retelling, not a
  lecture; the full version lives in "Background a modern reader needs."
- **Name-puns.** The text puns constantly on names; flag each in the retelling
  aside and gloss it in *Key original-language terms* (see Motifs §4).
- **Harmful reception.** Always framed and rejected, never given how-to detail —
  owned by the Reception-History Harm Historian (#11). Never softened.
- **Controlled tags.** Theme, genre, figure, and place **tags** come from the `R0`
  taxonomy (§3); this registry remains the source of truth for a figure's or place's
  **canonical form and disambiguation**, and `R0`'s figure facet defers to it. Keep
  the two in step: a new recurring figure/place gets a registry entry *and* a tag.

## 4 · Motifs & recurring images

| Motif | What it is | Recurs in |
|---|---|---|
| Fratricide / sibling rivalry | A brother harms or supplants a brother | Cain–Abel; Jacob–Esau; Joseph & brothers |
| The younger / unexpected one favored | God's choice overturns birth order or expectation | Abel; Isaac; Jacob; Joseph; David |
| Unmastered anger | Resentment that, unchecked, drives violence ("sin crouching at the door," Gen 4:7) | Cain–Abel; later kings |
| Blood crying from the ground | Innocent blood as a voice demanding justice | Cain–Abel (origin); echoed Heb 12:24; prophets |
| Mercy within judgment | Punishment and protection in the same act (Cain's mark) | Cain–Abel; the Flood (the ark); exile-and-return |
| Exile / wandering / restlessness | Estrangement from place and presence as consequence | Cain (Nod); expulsion from Eden; the Babylonian exile |
| Sacrifice; firstborn & fat | The best/most-costly portion given to God | Cain–Abel; Passover; Levitical law |
| The name-pun device | Names that encode meaning (Cain/*qanah*, Abel/*hevel*, Nod/"wandering", Adam/*ʾadamah*) | throughout the Hebrew Bible |

## 5 · Name-collision watchlist (disambiguate every time)

- **Two Enochs:** Cain's son (Gen 4:17) ≠ Enoch who "walked with God" and was taken (Gen 5:24).
- **Two Lamechs:** the violent boast-maker in Cain's line (Gen 4:18–24) ≠ Noah's father in Seth's line (Gen 5:25–31).
- **"East of Eden":** the narrative location *and* the cultural idiom (and the Steinbeck novel) — keep the three uses distinct.
