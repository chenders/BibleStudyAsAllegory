# A Scholarly Bible Companion: Reference Architecture for Six Parallel Corpora

*Update note (v2): originally scoped to five corpora and a four-axis rubric. Now
extended to **six corpora** — the **Book of Mormon** added as `R6` — and to the
project's current **five-axis** rubric (`I·C·P·H·A`, adding **Attestation**). The
five-corpus research findings below are unchanged; the Book of Mormon is summarized

## TL;DR
- This report delivers the research foundation and editorial blueprint for six distinct reference documents — the Protestant 66-book Bible (broken into pericope-level units with genre, cross-references, keyword taxonomy, and scoring evidence), the Torah in Jewish framing (54 parashot), the Quran (114 suras), the Hadith (Sunni Kutub al-Sittah + Shia Four Books), the Ancient Near Eastern source texts, and the Book of Mormon (15 internal books, in its own Latter Day Saint framing) — with verified structural data, named scholarly positions on contested questions, and concrete cultural-afterlife evidence for scoring.
- The single most important methodological finding: the strongest, best-attested ANE–Bible links are the Flood (Gilgamesh XI / Atrahasis), creation (Enuma Elish), law (Hammurabi/Covenant Code), wisdom (Amenemope/Proverbs 22–24; Ludlul/Job), Psalm 29/104 (Ugaritic Baal; Aten Hymn), and Cain & Abel (Dumuzid & Enkimdu); scholars dispute whether each represents direct borrowing, shared cultural matrix, or polemical response, and the report flags these positions rather than asserting one.
- For scoring, survey and art-historical data converge: a small set of episodes (Creation/Adam & Eve, Noah's Flood, the Crucifixion/Resurrection, the Annunciation, the Last Supper, David & Goliath) dominate both public familiarity and Western art, while a separate set (mark of Cain/curse of Ham, conquest texts) carries the heaviest harmful-reception burden — these axes must be scored independently.
## Key Findings

### 1. The deliverable is six documents, not one — and they must be structured differently
Each corpus has its own native organizing logic, and forcing them into a single schema would distort them. The Bible document is best atomized into **pericopes** (the scholarly term for a self-contained unit — scene, parable, law collection, oracle, psalm, or argument-section). The Torah document must follow the **54 parashot** cycle and Tanakh ordering. The Quran follows its **114 suras** with Meccan/Medinan classification. The Hadith document is organized by **collection → kitab (book/topic) → individual hadith with isnad grading**. The ANE document is organized **by text, each mapped to the biblical passage it parallels**. The Book of Mormon document follows its **15 internal books** (1 Nephi–Moroni), mediated through the three narrator-authors (Nephi, Mormon, Moroni), each broken into narrative/doctrinal units that link heavily back to the Bible. A shared keyword taxonomy and a shared scoring rubric cross-link all six.

### 2. Recommended pericope schema for every Bible unit
For each unit, capture: (1) Traditional title + descriptive subtitle; (2) Reference and unit boundaries (book ch:v–v); (3) Genre/form; (4) Substantive description of content and teaching; (5) Cross-references (intra-biblical parallels, e.g. Synoptic parallels, Kings/Chronicles doublets, four Passion accounts) and inter-corpus links; (6) Keyword/tag taxonomy; (7) Five independent scoring axes with evidence.

### 3. The five scoring axes, defined
- **Influence/afterlife** (art, literature, music, film, idiom, law/politics).
- **Contestedness** (textual cruxes, source-critical debate, literal-vs-figurative, denominational disputes).
- **Popularity/familiarity** (general-public recognition; survey-anchored).
- **Harmful reception** (documented misuse).
- **Attestation** (how far external evidence supports the events as historical — the compressed *Is it historical?* verdict; added after the original four).
  These must be scored separately: a unit can be hugely influential and popular yet uncontested (the Annunciation), or low-popularity yet high-harm (curse of Ham), or universally known yet make no testable historical claim at all (Eden).

## Details

### CORPUS 1 — THE PROTESTANT 66-BOOK BIBLE

**Canon structure:** 39 OT + 27 NT = 66 books. The Protestant OT contains the same books as the Jewish Tanakh but in a different order and division (the Tanakh's 24 books vs. the Protestant 39, achieved by splitting Samuel, Kings, Chronicles, Ezra–Nehemiah, and the Twelve Minor Prophets).

**Pentateuch/Torah narratives & law (Genesis–Deuteronomy).** Key pericopes with their strongest cross-corpus links:
- **Genesis 1:1–2:3 — The Seven-Day Creation** (genre: liturgical/cosmogonic narrative, Priestly source). Links to **Enuma Elish**: shared motifs of primordial watery chaos, the Hebrew *tehom* ("deep") being linguistically related to **Tiamat**, creation by separation/ordering, and darkness preceding creation. Scholarly positions range from direct dependence (older critical view) to shared ANE conceptual matrix with Genesis as polemical monotheistic response (BioLogos, Joseph Lam/Bart Ehrman blog). Contested: literal six-day vs. figurative reading is a major modern denominational fault line. **61% per the ABC News PrimeTime poll (telephone survey of 1,011 adults, Feb. 6–10, 2004, ±3 pts) believe "the account of creation in the Bible's book of Genesis is literally true."**
- **Genesis 2:4–3:24 — Eden and the Fall** (narrative, Yahwist source). Among the most-painted biblical scenes (Michelangelo's Sistine *Creation of Adam*; Rubens/Brueghel *Garden of Eden*). High popularity: **56% per Jonathan Hill's National Study of Religion & Human Origins (2014, funded by BioLogos), reported by Slate (Dec. 2014), affirmed that "Adam and Eve … were real, historical people" — though only 44% were "absolutely or very certain"** (Gallup's separate young-earth creationist figure was 42%, May 8–11, 2014, n=1,028). High contestedness (original sin doctrine; evolution debate).
- **Genesis 4:1–16 — Cain and Abel** (narrative). Links to Sumerian **Dumuzid and Enkimdu** ("Inanna Prefers the Farmer," ETCSL 4.0.8.3.3): farmer-vs-shepherd rivalry over divine favor; Samuel Noah Kramer drew the comparison. Note the key difference: in the Sumerian tale the rivalry is peaceful and the shepherd wins the goddess, whereas Genesis ends in fratricide — scholars see a shared *motif* (pastoral/agrarian tension) rather than direct borrowing. Also links to **Quran 5:27–32** (see Corpus 3). HARMFUL RECEPTION: the "mark of Cain" was misinterpreted as dark skin and, conflated with the "curse of Ham" (Gen 9:20–27), used to justify the Atlantic slave trade, segregation, and (in LDS history) priesthood exclusion — documented in David M. Goldenberg, *The Curse of Ham* (Princeton, 2003) and Ruth Mellinkoff, *The Mark of Cain* (1981).
- **Genesis 6–9 — Noah and the Flood** (narrative). The single strongest ANE parallel: **Gilgamesh Tablet XI** (Utnapishtim) and the older **Atrahasis Epic**, plus the Sumerian **Eridu Genesis**. Shared elements: divine decision to destroy humanity, a chosen man told to build a vessel, animals aboard, the flood, birds sent to test the waters, post-flood sacrifice "savored" by the deity, and a divine commitment against future floods. Alexander Heidel called these "the most remarkable parallels." Scholarly consensus (per Bible Interp, TheTorah.com): the Mesopotamian account is the source/background of the biblical one. **60% per the ABC News PrimeTime poll (Feb. 6–10, 2004) said "the story of Noah's ark, the global flood, and God's covenant to never destroy the Earth again" is literally true.** Hugely depicted (Edward Hicks's *Noah's Ark*; J.M.W. Turner).
- **Genesis 11:1–9 — Tower of Babel** (etiological narrative). Babylonian ziggurat backdrop.
- **Genesis 12–25 — Abraham cycle**, including **Genesis 22, the Binding of Isaac (Aqedah)** (narrative; one of the most theologically loaded and most-painted scenes — Caravaggio, Rembrandt). Parallels the Quranic near-sacrifice (Quran 37), where Islamic tradition generally identifies the son as Ishmael.
- **Genesis 37–50 — Joseph cycle**, including **Genesis 39, Joseph and Potiphar's wife**, which parallels the Egyptian **Tale of Two Brothers** (Papyrus D'Orbiney, 19th Dynasty, c. 1200 BCE): the spurned-seductress-false-accusation motif. Scholars (BAS Library) debate direct borrowing vs. a widespread folk motif; most see a shared narrative type rather than copying.
- **Exodus narratives:** the burning bush, the **Ten Plagues**, the **Passover** (Exod 12), the **Parting of the Red Sea** (Exod 14 — **64% per the ABC News PrimeTime poll (Feb. 6–10, 2004) believed "the story of Moses parting the Red Sea was literally true, meaning it happened that way word-for-word"**), the **Ten Commandments** (Exod 20 // Deut 5).
- **Legal codes — the Covenant Code (Exod 20:22–23:19), Holiness Code (Lev 17–26), Deuteronomic Code (Deut 12–26).** Strong links to the **Code of Hammurabi** (c. 1755 BCE): casuistic "if…then" form, *lex talionis* ("eye for eye," Exod 21:24 // CH 196–201), and the goring-ox laws (Exod 21:28–32 // CH 250–252). Key scholarly debate: David P. Wright (*Inventing God's Law*, 2009) argues the Covenant Code directly used and revised Hammurabi (dating its composition 740–640 BCE under Neo-Assyrian influence); Bruce Wells and others argue for shared legal tradition rather than literary dependence. Note the theological difference: Hammurabi grades penalties by social class; the Covenant Code applies them more equally and prioritizes human life. For long legal/census/genealogy sections, group sensibly rather than atomize.
  **Historical books (Joshua–Esther).** Joshua's conquest narratives carry HARMFUL RECEPTION (invoked for colonial conquest and holy-war ideology). The Kings/Chronicles relationship is a major intra-biblical cross-reference axis (Chronicles retells Samuel–Kings with a priestly/Davidic theological slant). The **Tel Dan Stele** (c. 840 BCE, "House of David"/*bytdwd*) provides the earliest widely accepted extra-biblical reference to the Davidic dynasty (paralleling 2 Kings 8–9); the **Mesha Stele** (c. 840 BCE, Moabite) parallels 2 Kings 3 and may also contain "House of David." David & Goliath (1 Sam 17) is a top-tier popularity/idiom unit ("David and Goliath" = underdog; Michelangelo, Caravaggio). Ruth and Esther are self-contained novellas; Esther anchors Purim.

**Wisdom & poetry.**
- **Job** — links to **Ludlul bēl nēmeqi** ("The Babylonian Job," Akkadian, composed late 2nd millennium BCE under the Kassite king Nazi-Maruttash) and the **Babylonian Theodicy** (c. 1000 BCE); both treat the righteous sufferer. Scholars (Takayoshi Oshima, *Babylonian Poems of Pious Sufferers*, 2014) note Job uniquely has God appear and answer, and the sufferer is more nearly righteous. Relationship is "shared theme/genre," not borrowing.
- **Psalms** — treat the Psalter as a 150-unit collection but break out the famous individual psalms: Ps 1, **Ps 22** (Passion), **Ps 23** (shepherd; top idiom/familiarity), **Ps 29** (strongest Ugaritic parallel — Yahweh as thunderous "rider on the clouds" echoing Baal's epithet, KTU 1.2 IV 8; the *bene elim* divine council), **Ps 51** (penitential), **Ps 104** (parallels the Egyptian **Great Hymn to the Aten**, c. 1350 BCE — shared imagery of day/night, animals, divine provision; scholars lean toward indirect/common literary tradition, per TheTorah.com), **Ps 110**, **Ps 137** (exile), **Ps 139**, **Ps 150**.
- **Proverbs** — **Proverbs 22:17–24:22** parallels the Egyptian **Instruction of Amenemope** (Ramesside, c. 1300–1075 BCE); the "thirty sayings" of Prov 22:20 corresponds to Amenemope's thirty chapters (Erman's emendation). Most scholars hold the Hebrew used the Egyptian (TheTorah.com, Emerton, Shupak), though direction and coincidence are debated.
- **Ecclesiastes** (skeptical wisdom; Gilgamesh's carpe-diem advice is a noted parallel), **Song of Songs** (love poetry; allegorized in both Jewish and Christian tradition).
  **Prophets (Isaiah–Malachi).** Break oracle-by-oracle at reasonable grain. Isaiah's "Servant Songs" (esp. Isa 53) are a major Christian cross-reference to the Passion. Daniel's apocalyptic (esp. Dan 7, 12) reflects Persian/Zoroastrian-era eschatology and angelology (named angels Michael, Gabriel; resurrection) — see Corpus 5. Jonah is a self-contained narrative parodying prophecy.

**Gospels and the Synoptic Problem.** Matthew, Mark, Luke are the "Synoptics" (arrangeable in a synopsis); John is distinct. The dominant scholarly solution is the **Two-Source Hypothesis**: Markan priority (Mark written first — per Robert H. Stein, **"97.2 percent of the words in Mark have a parallel in Matthew, and 88.4 percent have a parallel in Luke"**) plus a hypothetical sayings source **Q** for the ~200+ "double tradition" verses in Matthew and Luke not in Mark. Material in all three = "triple tradition"; Matthew-only = M, Luke-only = L. Main rival hypotheses: the **Farrer Hypothesis** (Britain; dispenses with Q, Luke used Matthew) and the **Two-Gospel/Griesbach Hypothesis** (America; Matthean priority). The editorial system should tag each Gospel pericope by tradition (triple/double/M/L/John) and link Synoptic parallels and the four Passion/Resurrection accounts. Most-depicted scenes (Annunciation, Nativity, Last Supper, Crucifixion, Descent from the Cross, Resurrection) cluster the highest art-influence scores; the Byzantine "Twelve Great Feasts" cycle is a useful art-historical taxonomy.

**Acts, Epistles, Revelation.** Acts = narrative (Pentecost, conversions, Paul's journeys). Break Pauline and general epistles into argument-sections (e.g. Romans: 1:18–3:20 universal sin; 3:21–5:21 justification; 6–8 sanctification; 9–11 Israel; 12–15 exhortation). Revelation = apocalyptic, broken vision-by-vision (seven churches, seven seals, seven trumpets, the woman and dragon, the beast, Babylon's fall, the millennium, New Jerusalem).

### CORPUS 2 — THE TORAH (Jewish framing)

The Five Books: **Bereshit** (Genesis), **Shemot** (Exodus), **Vayikra** (Leviticus), **Bamidbar** (Numbers), **Devarim** (Deuteronomy). Each named from its opening Hebrew word. The Torah is divided into **54 weekly parashot** read on an annual cycle beginning and ending at **Simchat Torah** (the final portion, **V'Zot HaBerachah**, Deut 33–34, is always read then). The custom dates to the Babylonian captivity; in shorter years some portions are doubled to fit ~50–54 available Shabbatot. Each parashah takes its name from its first distinctive word (e.g. Bereshit, Noach, Lech-Lecha, Vayera, Chayei Sarah, Toldot, Vayetze, Vayishlach, Vayeshev, Miketz, Vayigash, Vayechi for Genesis). Each is paired with a **Haftarah** (prophetic reading). The Torah document should present the parashah list with references, then map the major narrative and legal units onto the parashot, and note key **midrashic/Talmudic** interpretive traditions (e.g. Genesis Rabbah XXII.12 on Cain's leprous "mark"; rabbinic readings of the Aqedah; the Mishnah Sanhedrin 4:5 teaching on saving a life). Crucially, frame the Tanakh's three-part structure — **Torah, Nevi'im (Prophets), Ketuvim (Writings)** — and note where Jewish ordering and division differ from the Christian OT (e.g. Chronicles ends the Tanakh, not Malachi).

### CORPUS 3 — THE QURAN

**114 suras**, ordered roughly longest-to-shortest (not chronologically), each divided into *ayat* (verses); the text is also divided into **30 ajza' (juz')** for recitation. Suras are classified **Meccan** (pre-Hijra; shorter, poetic, focused on tawhid/monotheism, judgment, prophetic stories) or **Medinan** (post-Hijra; longer, legislative, community/social law, addressing People of the Book). The consensus count is commonly given as ~86 Meccan and ~28 Medinan, with ~12 disputed suras (including Al-Fatiha, Al-Rahman, Al-Ikhlas, Al-Falaq, Al-Nas). Classification criteria include verse length, the address "O mankind" (Meccan) vs. "O you who believe" (Medinan), and prostration verses.

**Shared figures and key differences from the Bible:**
- **Adam** — Iblis's refusal to prostrate (a Second Temple/apocryphal narrative motif).
- **Nuh (Noah)** — the Flood; one of Nuh's sons drowns refusing to board (not in Genesis).
- **Ibrahim (Abraham)** — smashes idols; near-sacrifice of his son (Sura 37), generally Ishmael in later tradition.
- **Lut (Lot), Yusuf (Joseph) — Sura 12** (111 verses), narrated in full chronological order, unique among Quranic prophet stories; the Quran emphasizes divine sovereignty and Joseph's prophetic/preaching role, interrupts with divine commentary (reducing narrative suspense vs. Genesis), and differs on details (Jacob perceives the dreams' significance; Joseph is found by travelers rather than sold by brothers in one reading). Sura 12 was revealed late in the Meccan period, reportedly in a single sitting.
- **Musa (Moses)** — the most-mentioned figure; Exodus events.
- **Dawud (David), Sulayman (Solomon)** — kingship, wisdom, Solomon's command of jinn/animals.
- **Yunus (Jonah)** — Sura 10/the fish.
- **Maryam (Mary) — Sura 19** — the virgin birth of Isa; Mary is the only woman named in the Quran.
- **Isa (Jesus)** — a prophet, not divine; born of a virgin, performs miracles, but the crucifixion is denied/altered (4:157).
- **The two sons of Adam — Quran 5:27–32** (Surah al-Ma'ida): the rejected-vs-accepted sacrifice and the murder, PLUS a detail absent from Genesis — **a crow/raven scratches the ground to teach the murderer to bury his brother's corpse** (5:31). The Quran does not name the sons; the names **Qabil (Cain)** and **Habil (Abel)** come from later tafsir/qisas al-anbiya literature. **Verse 5:32** (Sahih International: *"…We decreed upon the Children of Israel that whoever kills a soul unless for a soul or for corruption [done] in the land - it is as if he had slain mankind entirely. And whoever saves one - it is as if he had saved mankind entirely"*) is explicitly framed as a decree "upon the Children of Israel" and closely parallels **Mishnah Sanhedrin 4:5** (Herbert Danby's translation: *"…if any man has caused a single soul to perish… Scripture imputes it to him as though he had caused a whole world to perish; and if any man saves alive a single soul… as though he had saved alive a whole world"*). The Quran universalizes the rabbinic teaching (which in many manuscripts reads "a single soul of Israel"). Note that much online discussion of this parallel is polemical; the reliable anchors are Danby's Mishnah translation and the Sahih International/Abdel Haleem Quran wording.
  **Major theological themes:** *tawhid* (oneness of God), prophecy, resurrection and final judgment, law (*sharia*). Most-recited/influential suras: Al-Fatiha (recited in every prayer), Al-Baqarah (Ayat al-Kursi 2:255), Ya-Sin (36), Al-Rahman (55), Al-Ikhlas (112), the closing Al-Falaq and Al-Nas.

### CORPUS 4 — THE HADITH

**What hadith are:** recorded sayings, actions, or tacit approvals of the Prophet Muhammad. Each has two parts: the **isnad** (chain of narrators) and the **matn** (text). After the Quran, hadith are the second source of Islamic law and theology.

**Sunni — the Kutub al-Sittah ("Six Books"),** compiled c. 840–912 CE: **Sahih al-Bukhari** (d. 870), **Sahih Muslim** (d. 875) — the two most authoritative, containing only *sahih* material; **Sunan Abu Dawud** (d. 889), **Jami al-Tirmidhi** (d. 892), **Sunan al-Nasa'i** (d. 915), **Sunan Ibn Majah** (d. 887/889). The "six" grouping was formalized by Ibn al-Qaisarani (11th c.), who added Ibn Majah; some (Malikis, Ibn al-Athir) substitute Malik's **Muwatta**. Even the two Sahihs faced targeted critiques (al-Daraqutni, d. 995, flagged 78 hadith in Bukhari, 100 in Muslim).

**Shia — the Four Books (al-Kutub al-Arba'a):** **al-Kafi** by al-Kulayni (d. 329/941) — containing roughly 16,000 narrations (sources give 16,099–16,202 by edition), divided into **Usul al-Kafi** (doctrine/theology, ~3,783 traditions), **Furu al-Kafi** (jurisprudence, ~11,156), and **Rawdat al-Kafi** (miscellany, ~600); **Man la yahduruhu al-Faqih** by al-Shaykh al-Saduq/Ibn Babawayh (d. 381/991); **Tahdhib al-Ahkam** and **al-Istibsar**, both by al-Shaykh al-Tusi (d. 460/1067). The collective label "Four Books" is a later (15th–17th c.) designation.

**Grading (isnad science):** Sunni classical grading is three-fold — **sahih** (authentic), **hasan** (good), **da'if** (weak); only sahih and hasan are evidentiary in law, while da'if may be cited for moral encouragement. Shia grading is four/five-fold — **sahih** (all narrators reliable AND Imami/Ja'fari), **hasan**, **muwaththaq** (a reliable but non-Imami narrator in the chain; the *muwaththaq* category was introduced by Ibn Tawus, d. 664/1265), and **da'if**. The decisive Shia distinction is the sectarian affiliation (Imami status) of narrators and transmission through the infallible Imams (especially Ja'far al-Sadiq). Note: Usuli Shia scholars hold the Four Books authoritative but not wholly infallible (each hadith examined individually), whereas Akhbaris accept their entire contents.

**Thematic organization:** collections are arranged by *kitab* (book/topic) — faith, purification, prayer (salat), almsgiving (zakat), fasting (sawm), pilgrimage (hajj), transactions, marriage, etc. Famous individual hadith: the **Hadith of Gabriel** (defining islam/iman/ihsan), the **opening hadith of Bukhari** ("actions are by intentions"), the **Forty Hadith of al-Nawawi** anthology, and **Hadith Qudsi** (divine sayings). Classical commentaries: Ibn Hajar's *Fath al-Bari* (on Bukhari), al-Nawawi's *Sharh* (on Muslim). Hadith supplement the Quran by specifying practice (e.g. the mechanics of prayer) and contain narratives paralleling biblical material (Isra/Mi'raj; stories of earlier prophets — *qisas al-anbiya*).

### CORPUS 5 — ANCIENT NEAR EASTERN & RELATED SOURCE TEXTS

Organize each by: what it is, date/culture/language, discovery/preservation, contents, the biblical passage paralleled, and the scholarly view on the relationship (direct borrowing / common tradition / polemic / coincidence — with contestation noted).

**Mesopotamian:**
- **Epic of Gilgamesh, Tablet XI** (Standard Babylonian version, Akkadian; the flood interpolated by Sin-leqi-unnini from Atrahasis; tablets from Ashurbanipal's library at Nineveh, 7th c. BCE) — Noah's Flood (Gen 6–9). View: source/background. The most cited ANE–Bible parallel.
- **Atrahasis Epic** (Old Babylonian, c. 17th c. BCE; a tablet at the Morgan Library) — flood + creation of humans to relieve the gods' labor.
- **Enuma Elish** (Babylonian "creation," 7 tablets, Akkadian; discovered by Layard at Nineveh 1849, translated 1876) — Genesis 1 (chaos waters, Tiamat/*tehom*, ordering of cosmos). Recited at the Babylonian New Year. View: shared matrix / possible polemic.
- **Eridu Genesis / Sumerian Flood Story** — Sumerian flood + antediluvian kings.
- **Sumerian King List** — long-reigning antediluvian kings ↔ the long-lived patriarchs of Genesis 5.
- **Descent of Inanna/Ishtar** — underworld/dying-and-rising motifs.
- **Dumuzid and Enkimdu** ("Inanna Prefers the Farmer") — Cain & Abel (shepherd vs. farmer; Kramer's comparison).
- **Law codes: Ur-Nammu** (c. 2100 BCE, earliest known), **Lipit-Ishtar**, **Laws of Eshnunna**, **Code of Hammurabi** (c. 1755 BCE, Louvre stele) — biblical law (Covenant Code, *lex talionis*, goring ox). View: Wright argues direct use; Wells argues common tradition.
- **Ludlul bēl nēmeqi** and the **Babylonian Theodicy** — Job.
- **Mesopotamian wisdom** generally ↔ Proverbs/Ecclesiastes.
  **Egyptian:**
- **Instruction of Amenemope** (Ramesside, c. 1300–1075 BCE) — Proverbs 22:17–24:22.
- **Tale of Two Brothers** (Papyrus D'Orbiney, 19th Dynasty, c. 1200 BCE, British Museum) — Joseph & Potiphar's wife (Gen 39).
- **Great Hymn to the Aten** (Amarna, c. 1350 BCE, tomb of Ay) — Psalm 104. View: indirect/common tradition.
- Egyptian creation (Memphite/Heliopolitan; creation by speech) and afterlife texts ↔ Genesis 1 speech-creation.
  **Levantine/Canaanite:**
- **Ugaritic texts** (Ras Shamra, discovered 1929; 14th–13th c. BCE; Ugaritic cuneiform alphabet): the **Baal Cycle** (KTU 1.1–1.6; Baal vs. Yamm/Mot; divine names El, Baal, Asherah; the divine council; Baal "rider of the clouds") — Psalm 29, Leviathan/Litan (Job 41, Ps 74), Deut 32:8–9 (sons of El). The **Legend of Keret** and **Tale of Aqhat** (Keret loses seven sons ↔ Job motif). View: shared West Semitic heritage; Psalm 29 may repurpose Canaanite storm-god language as polemic (Craigie).
- **Merneptah Stele** (Egyptian, c. 1207 BCE, discovered by Petrie 1896) — earliest undisputed extra-biblical mention of "Israel."
- **Mesha Stele / Moabite Stone** (c. 840 BCE) — 2 Kings 3; possible "House of David."
- **Tel Dan Stele** (c. 840 BCE, Old Aramaic, discovered 1993) — "House of David" (*bytdwd*); parallels 2 Kings 8–9. A minority disputes the reading (no word-divider).
- **Amarna letters** (14th c. BCE) — Canaanite city-states; the *Habiru*.
  **Persian/Zoroastrian (bridging):** The exile (586–538 BCE) and Persian rule are the proposed contact point for developments in Jewish **angelology** (named angels Michael, Gabriel), **demonology**, **dualism** (light vs. darkness — cf. the Dead Sea War Scroll), **eschatology/resurrection**, and the evolution of *ha-satan* from a heavenly accuser (Job) to an adversarial figure. Most strongly visible in Daniel, 1 Enoch, Jubilees. Scholarly debate: some (the Jewish Encyclopedia tradition; Shaked) affirm substantial Zoroastrian influence; others find the link unproven owing to dating uncertainties for Zoroastrian texts.

**Second Temple / intertestamental (bridging category):** **1 Enoch** (Watchers, apocalyptic), **Jubilees** (rewritten Genesis/Exodus), the **Dead Sea Scrolls** (Qumran; oldest biblical manuscripts, sectarian dualism), and the **Septuagint** (the Greek OT — a textual witness often differing from the Masoretic Text, and the OT most quoted in the NT). Mark these as bridging Judaism, the NT, and later traditions.

### CORPUS 6 — THE BOOK OF MORMON

**Status:** added after the original five; lives as `R6`, framed in its own right (Latter Day Saint reception) rather than as an appendix to the Bible. Presented by believers as "Another Testament of Jesus Christ."

**Structure:** 15 internal books — 1 Nephi, 2 Nephi, Jacob, Enos, Jarom, Omni, Words of Mormon, Mosiah, Alma, Helaman, 3 Nephi, 4 Nephi, Mormon, Ether, Moroni — mediated through three narrator-authors (**Nephi**, **Mormon**, **Moroni**; the framework in Grant Hardy, *Understanding the Book of Mormon*, Oxford 2010). Two record-streams interleave: the small plates of Nephi (1 Nephi–Omni, first-person) and Mormon's abridgment of the large plates (Mosiah onward); Ether is Moroni's abridgment-within-the-abridgment of the far older Jaredites. Organized like the Bible document — **by internal book → narrative/doctrinal unit**, each with description, cross-references, tags, and a five-axis score.

**Cross-links to the Bible (the spine of the comparative work):** large quoted/echoed blocks — ~19–21 chapters of Isaiah (2 Ne 12–24 ≈ Isaiah 2–14; Isa 48–51, 53 elsewhere); the Sermon on the Mount (3 Ne 12–14 ≈ Matthew 5–7); Malachi 3–4 (3 Ne 24–25); the olive-tree allegory (Jacob 5 ↔ Romans 11); the Babel frame (Ether 1 ↔ Genesis 11); and pervasive exodus typology. The `→bom` tag in `R0`/`R1` makes these links bidirectional.

**Origins and historicity (three attributed voices, per the project's neutrality rule):** (a) the **LDS faith account** — golden plates revealed by the angel Moroni, translated by Joseph Smith, first published 1830; (b) the **mainstream academic/scientific consensus** — a 19th-century work, citing anachronisms (horses, steel, wheat), absence of archaeological/genetic/linguistic corroboration, and KJV dependence (including the italics, and the Deutero-Isaiah dating problem); (c) **LDS apologetics** — limited-geography (Mesoamerican) models, chiasmus (Alma 36), Hebraisms, Arabian "Lehi's trail." Intra-movement difference is noted (LDS Church holds historicity essential; the Community of Christ takes no official position). The **Attestation (A)** axis encodes voice (b): datable ancient-event units score A1, doctrine/sermon/vision/quotation score A0, while the faith and apologetic voices stay in the prose.

**Harmful reception (documented, framed, never endorsed):** the racial-curse passages (2 Ne 5:21; Alma 3; 3 Ne 2:15; Jacob 3:5–9) were used to support the LDS priesthood/temple ban (1852–1978); the Church's 2013 "Race and the Priesthood" essay disavows them. ## Recommendations

**Stage 1 — Build the spine (now).** Lock the six-document architecture and the pericope schema. Generate the master lists first, because they are finite and verifiable: the 66-book canon, the 54 parashot, the 114 suras (with Meccan/Medinan tags and juz' boundaries), the hadith collections with their kitab structure, the ANE text inventory, and the Book of Mormon's 15 internal books. Adopt a controlled keyword vocabulary (themes, motifs, figures, places, genre tags) shared across all six.

**Stage 2 — Populate pericopes with cross-references.** Tag every Gospel unit by Synoptic tradition (triple/double/M/L/John); link every Kings unit to its Chronicles parallel; link every ANE-parallel unit to Corpus 5. Use **named scholarly sources** for contested claims (e.g., Wright vs. Wells on Hammurabi; Two-Source vs. Farrer on the Synoptics) and attribute positions rather than asserting them.

**Stage 3 — Score on five independent axes.** Anchor **popularity** in survey data. The verified ABC News PrimeTime poll (Feb. 6–10, 2004, n=1,011, ±3 pts) gives literal-belief rates of **Creation 61%, Noah's Flood 60%, Red Sea parting 64%**; the Hill/BioLogos 2014 study gives **56% historical Adam & Eve**. (The Barna organization separately reported higher literal-belief rates for the Resurrection and for Daniel in the lions' den; re-source these to the original Barna release before citing, as they were not independently re-verified here.) Anchor **influence** in concrete named artworks/idioms (most-depicted: Creation of Adam, Annunciation, Last Supper, Crucifixion, Descent from the Cross; idioms: "good Samaritan," "prodigal son," "David and Goliath," "scapegoat," "the writing on the wall"). Anchor **contestedness** in documented textual cruxes and denominational disputes. Anchor **harm** in documented misuse (mark of Cain/curse of Ham for slavery and segregation, citing Goldenberg and Mellinkoff; conquest texts for colonialism; texts misused against women and LGBTQ people). Anchor **attestation** in archaeology, inscriptions, and scientific findings (or their absence) — the same evidence the *Is it historical?* field weighs — kept strictly separate from contestedness.

**Thresholds that would change the plan:** If editorial bandwidth is limited, atomize only the high-influence/high-popularity units fully and group the rest (genealogies, census lists, repetitive legal sections, minor prophetic oracles). If a parallel's scholarly status is genuinely unsettled (e.g., Aten/Ps 104 direction of influence), present both positions rather than scoring contestedness as resolved. Prioritize sourcing from the **Encyclopedia of the Bible and Its Reception**, **Blackwell Bible Commentaries** (reception history), **ANET (Pritchard)** and **COS (Hallo)** for ANE primary texts, and standard study Bibles/commentaries.

## Caveats
- **Source quality:** Much online material on ANE–Bible parallels comes from apologetics sites (in both directions — young-earth creationist and polemical). This report has preferred academic and reference sources (TheTorah.com, Biblical Archaeology Society, Cambridge/Harvard journals, university repositories, Oshima, Goldenberg, Wright) and flags where a claim rests on contested or non-neutral sourcing.
- **"Influence" claims about art** are based on art-historical surveys naming recurring subjects; a rigorous influence score would ideally draw on a catalogued database (e.g. a museum collection count) rather than listicles.
- **Survey data** on biblical literalism/familiarity is mostly US-centric and somewhat dated (2004 ABC, 2014 Hill/BioLogos, Barna); familiarity in secular/global contexts differs and should not be over-generalized. The Resurrection (75%) and Daniel (65%) figures attributed to Barna in early drafting were not independently re-verified and should be confirmed against the original Barna report.
- **Contested datings and authorship** (Pentateuchal source criticism, Q's existence, dating of Job, direction of the Amenemope and Aten parallels, the Tel Dan "House of David" reading, the extent of Zoroastrian influence) are genuinely unsettled; the report names the main positions rather than adjudicating them.
- **Harmful reception** is reported strictly as documented misuse of texts, not as the texts' inherent meaning.
- Some structural counts vary slightly by edition/tradition (e.g., al-Kafi's narration total is given as ~16,099–16,202; Meccan/Medinan sura counts vary by a dozen disputed cases); the report notes the ranges.
