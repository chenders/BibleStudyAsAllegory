# R0 — Scoring Rubric & Keyword Taxonomy

The shared measuring system for every corpus reference file in this project
(`R1` Bible, `R2` Torah, `R3` Quran, `R4` Hadith, `R5` Ancient Near Eastern
sources). Each story/unit in those files carries a compact score line built from
this rubric, plus tags drawn from this controlled vocabulary.

**What this is for.** To let a reader compare units at a glance — *which stories
are most influential, most contested, most familiar, most misused* — and to keep
those judgments consistent across ~1,000 entries and five corpora. The scores are
**editorial judgments anchored to evidence**, not precise measurements; they are
meant to be defensible, comparable, and refreshable, not exact.

**Read these as five independent axes.** A unit can be hugely influential and
familiar yet barely contested (the Annunciation). Another can be obscure to the
public yet carry a heavy harm history (the Curse of Ham). A third can be
universally known yet make no testable historical claim at all (Eden). Never
collapse them into one number that hides those differences.
 
---

## 1 · The five axes (each 0–5)

Each entry is scored on all five. The compact form used in the corpus files is:

`Influence I# · Contested C# · Popularity P# · Harm H# · Attestation A#`

e.g. `I5 · C4 · P5 · H2 · A0` (Cain and Abel).

### Axis 1 — Influence / Cultural Afterlife (I)
*How deep a mark the unit has left on art, literature, music, film/stage, everyday
language, and law/politics.* Anchored in **named, datable works and idioms**, not
vague "inspired many" claims.

- **I0 — None traceable.** No identifiable afterlife (e.g. a census list, a minor genealogical note).
- **I1 — Slight.** A scattered allusion or two; nothing a general reader would recognize.
- **I2 — Modest.** A known motif within specialist or devotional circles; some art or hymnody.
- **I3 — Substantial.** Repeatedly depicted or set; a recognizable theme; perhaps a minor idiom.
- **I4 — Major.** A staple of Western (or relevant) culture — many named artworks, literary retellings, musical settings, films; a live idiom.
- **I5 — Foundational.** A culture-shaping unit: among the most-painted/most-set scenes, a saturating idiom, recurring across art + literature + music + film + politics. (Creation, the Fall, the Flood, David & Goliath, the Crucifixion, the Prodigal Son.)
  *Evidence to cite:* specific artists/works, named literary retellings, musical
  settings (oratorio/opera/hymn), films, idioms ("scapegoat," "good Samaritan"),
  and uses in law or political rhetoric.

### Axis 2 — Contestedness (C)
*How much genuine, live disagreement the unit carries* — across textual criticism,
source/composition debate, literal-vs-figurative reading, and denominational or
inter-traditional dispute. This measures **disputed-ness, not difficulty**; a hard
poem everyone reads the same way scores low.

- **C0 — Settled.** No meaningful dispute over text, composition, or reading.
- **C1 — Minor.** A textual variant or scholarly footnote; nothing doctrine hinges on.
- **C2 — Noticeable.** A real crux or a modest interpretive spread, but no major fault line.
- **C3 — Significant.** A recognized scholarly debate (dating, sources) or a clear literal-vs-figurative split.
- **C4 — Sharp.** A fault line that divides traditions or schools (e.g. Genesis 1 literalism; justification by faith).
- **C5 — Defining.** A unit a whole division or doctrine turns on — the text *itself* is a battleground (e.g. "this is my body"; "on this rock"; the Servant Songs).
  *Evidence to cite:* the specific crux (verse + manuscript), the named schools/positions, the denominational or inter-traditional split.

### Axis 3 — Popularity / Familiarity (P)
*How well-known the unit is to a general (esp. broadly Western/Abrahamic-literate)
public* — independent of whether scholars find it interesting. Survey-anchored
where data exists; otherwise estimated from idiom penetration and cultural ubiquity.

- **P0 — Obscure.** Unknown outside specialists (most genealogies, minor oracles).
- **P1 — Low.** Recognized by attentive readers of the text, not the public.
- **P2 — Some.** Familiar in religious-education settings; a churchgoer would know it.
- **P3 — Broad.** Many non-religious people would recognize the story or its phrase.
- **P4 — High.** A household story; named in general culture, taught widely, idiomatic.
- **P5 — Universal.** "Everyone knows it," religious or not (Adam & Eve, Noah's Ark, Christmas/Nativity, the Crucifixion).
  *Survey anchors (US, dated — refresh at review):* ABC News PrimeTime poll
  (Feb 6–10, 2004; n=1,011; ±3) found literal-belief rates of **Creation 61%,
  Noah's Flood 60%, Red Sea parting 64%**; Hill/BioLogos (2014) found **~56%**
  affirming a historical Adam & Eve. These measure *belief in historicity*, which
  correlates with but is not identical to *familiarity*; use as a proxy and label the source.

### Axis 4 — Harmful Reception (H)
*The documented history of the unit being used to cause harm* — antisemitism,
racism/slavery, holy war/colonial conquest, misogyny, anti-LGBTQ application,
witch-hunting, etc. **This axis rates the harm history, not the text's plain
meaning.** A high H is a flag for careful framing, never an endorsement; per the
project's standing rules, harmful readings are always recorded as documented
misuse, framed and rejected, never given how-to detail.

- **H0 — None recorded.** No notable harmful reception.
- **H1 — Isolated.** Rare or fringe misuse; little historical weight.
- **H2 — Documented.** A real, sourced misuse history of limited scope or now-marginal. (Cain's "mark" racialized.)
- **H3 — Significant.** A misuse that shaped attitudes or some policy in a region/era.
- **H4 — Severe.** A reading that scaffolded large-scale, sustained harm (slavery, sustained antisemitism, conquest).
- **H5 — Catastrophic.** A text central to mass violence or systemic oppression across centuries (conquest/holy-war texts; "his blood be on us"; Curse of Ham + Atlantic slavery).
  *Evidence to cite, on the project's skeleton:* **Text → Reception → Consequence →
  Counter-tradition → Status**, with a confidence label (*well-attested /
  contested / speculative*) and a graded causal verb (*originated · provided
  scaffolding for · amplified · instrumentalized · contested · de-escalated*).

### Axis 5 — Historical Attestation (A)
*How far history, archaeology, and external evidence support the events as having
happened* — the compressed form of the entry's **Is it historical?** verdict. This
rates the **event**, and is kept strictly separate from Contestedness (which rates
scholarly/interpretive *dispute*) and from the text's theological meaning. Higher is
not "better": a symbolic story scores low because it makes no historical claim, not
because it fails one. Maps to the entry field's **well-attested / contested /
rejected** grading.

- **A0 — No testable claim.** Symbolic, etiological, or primeval material that doesn't assert a datable event (Creation, Eden, Cain and Abel, the parables).
- **A1 — Rejected as history.** The event as literally described is contradicted by the evidence (a global flood; a young earth; the sun standing still).
- **A2 — Legendary / unverifiable.** A figure or event possibly rooted in something real but beyond the reach of evidence (the patriarchs as individuals; an Exodus at the biblical scale).
- **A3 — Contested.** Genuine debate with real evidence on more than one side (the extent of a united monarchy; aspects of the conquest/settlement).
- **A4 — Substantially attested.** The core is supported by external or archaeological evidence, with details debated (the Babylonian exile; later-monarchic figures and events).
- **A5 — Externally corroborated.** Fixed by outside evidence — figures or events named in datable inscriptions or firmly set in a documented period (later Judahite kings; the Roman-era setting of the Gospels and Acts).
  *Evidence to cite:* the archaeological find, inscription, or scientific finding (or
  its absence); name what would count for or against, and label confidence. Cross-link
  to `R5` where an ANE source bears on the question.

## 2 · How to assign and present a score

1. **Score each axis on its own evidence.** Don't let a high Influence inflate Popularity, or a high Harm darken Influence.
2. **Anchor, don't guess.** Name at least one concrete piece of evidence per non-zero axis in the entry body (a work, an idiom, a debate, a documented misuse).
3. **Whole-text vs. this-unit.** Survey numbers usually measure the Bible *in general* or a whole story; keep that distinct from a single verse's profile and say which you mean.
4. **Date and refresh.** Tag survey-derived Popularity figures with their source and year; flag for refresh at lock.
5. **Confidence.** Where a score rests on thin or contested evidence, add `(low confidence)` after it.
6. **No composite by default.** If a single "prominence" sort is ever needed, use `I + P` (cultural weight) and report `C`, `H`, and `A` separately — never average harm or historicity into prominence.
   **Worked example — Cain and Abel (Gen 4:1–16):** `I5 · C4 · P5 · H2 · A0`.
   *I5* — Steinbeck's *East of Eden*, Byron's *Cain*, Grendel's descent in *Beowulf*,
   Scarlatti's *Il primo omicidio*, the idioms "am I my brother's keeper," "mark of
   Cain," "raising Cain." *C4* — the 4:7 and 4:8 cruxes, the 4:15 MT/LXX split, and a
   sharp literal-vs-figurative divide in the primeval history. *P5* — a universally
   known story. *H2* — the racialized "mark" and the Augustine "Cain = the Jews" trope,
   both documented misuses, framed and rejected. *A0* — primeval/etiological; makes no
   testable historical claim (see the entry's *Is it historical?*). (See the `R1` entry.)

### Who scores what
Each axis is assigned by the specialist who owns its evidence, then integrated by the
Managing Editor into the entry's score line:
- **I (Influence)** → Reception & Culture (#7).
- **C (Contestedness)** → Historical-Critical (#3), with Schism & Sectarian-Formation (#12) on the battleground (C5) texts.
- **P (Popularity)** → Sociologist of Religion (#8), who owns the survey anchors.
- **H (Harm)** → Reception-History Harm Historian (#11), whose Text→Reception→Consequence skeleton this axis is built on.
- **A (Attestation)** → Ancient-World Historian (#4) with the Historical-Critical scholar (#3) — the same pair who own the *Is it historical?* field.
  The **Managing Editor (#1)** integrates the five, keeps `R1` consistent, and guards the taxonomy.

## 3 · Controlled keyword taxonomy

Tag every unit with terms drawn from these five facets, so units cross-search
consistently across all corpora. Use existing terms before coining new ones; add a
new term to this list the first time it's genuinely needed.

### Facet A — Genre / form
`narrative` · `myth-of-origins` (etiology) · `genealogy` · `law` · `ritual-instruction` ·
`covenant` · `poetry` · `psalm` · `lament` · `hymn` · `wisdom` · `proverb` ·
`parable` · `oracle` · `prophecy` · `apocalyptic` · `vision` · `gospel-narrative` ·
`miracle` · `teaching-discourse` · `epistle-argument` · `letter` · `liturgy` ·
`travel-narrative` · `martyrdom` · `census-list`

### Facet B — Theme / motif
`creation` · `fall` · `sin` · `judgment` · `flood` · `covenant` · `promise` ·
`call` · `exile` · `wandering` · `exodus-deliverance` · `wilderness` · `law-giving` ·
`sacrifice` · `firstborn` · `sibling-rivalry` · `fratricide` · `younger-favored` ·
`trickster` · `barren-woman-births` · `dream-revelation` · `theophany` ·
`testing` · `faith` · `obedience` · `disobedience` · `kingship` · `anointing` ·
`underdog` · `wisdom-vs-folly` · `suffering-righteous` · `theodicy` · `repentance` ·
`mercy-within-judgment` · `blood-cries-out` · `remnant` · `messianic-hope` ·
`resurrection` · `incarnation` · `discipleship` · `reversal` · `kingdom-of-God` ·
`justification` · `love` · `martyrdom` · `end-times` · `new-creation`

### Facet C — Figures
Use the canonical English form (per the project's Recurring-Elements Registry):
`Adam` · `Eve` · `Cain` · `Abel` · `Noah` · `Abraham` · `Sarah` · `Hagar` ·
`Isaac` · `Ishmael` · `Jacob` · `Esau` · `Joseph` · `Moses` · `Aaron` · `Joshua` ·
`Samson` · `Ruth` · `Samuel` · `Saul` · `David` · `Solomon` · `Elijah` · `Elisha` ·
`Isaiah` · `Jeremiah` · `Ezekiel` · `Daniel` · `Jonah` · `Job` · `Esther` ·
`Mary` · `Joseph-of-Nazareth` · `John-the-Baptist` · `Jesus` · `Peter` · `Paul` ·
`Judas` · `Pilate` · `the-Twelve` · `YHWH/God` *(add figures as books are drafted)*

### Facet D — Places
`Eden` · `Nod` · `Ararat` · `Babel` · `Ur` · `Haran` · `Canaan` · `Egypt` ·
`Sodom` · `Moriah` · `Sinai/Horeb` · `wilderness` · `Jericho` · `Jerusalem` ·
`Bethlehem` · `Babylon` · `Nazareth` · `Galilee` · `Gethsemane` · `Golgotha` ·
`Patmos` *(add as needed)*

### Facet E — Cross-corpus link
Tag when a unit has a counterpart in another file:
`→torah` · `→quran` · `→hadith` · `→ANE:<text>` (e.g. `→ANE:Gilgamesh`,
`→ANE:Enuma-Elish`, `→ANE:Hammurabi`, `→ANE:Amenemope`, `→ANE:Aten-Hymn`,
`→ANE:Dumuzid-Enkimdu`, `→ANE:Atrahasis`, `→ANE:Baal-Cycle`, `→ANE:child-sacrifice`).
