# R6 — The Book of Mormon: Reference Companion

A book-by-book, story-by-story reference to the Book of Mormon — what each unit is,
where it sits, what it says, what it connects to (inside the book, to the Bible, and
across the companion corpora), a keyword taxonomy, and a five-axis score per unit.
Framed in its own right: as the text is received within the **Latter Day Saint (LDS /
"Mormon") movement** — a scripture believers call "Another Testament of Jesus Christ,"
not an appendix to the Bible.

**Companion files:** `R0` scoring rubric & taxonomy · `R1` Bible · `R2` Torah ·
`R3` Quran · `R4` Hadith · `R5` Ancient Near Eastern sources. Cross-corpus links use
`→R1`, `→R5`, etc. Five-axis scores follow the `R0` rubric and are scored here (the
Book of Mormon has no counterpart catalogue elsewhere to defer to).
 
---

## How to read an entry

> **Title — descriptive subtitle** · `Book ch:v–v` · *genre*
> What the unit contains and says (a few sentences).
> **Related:** internal & cross-corpus links. **Keywords:** taxonomy tags. **Score:** `I# · C# · P# · H# · A#`

**Score key** (full definitions in `R0`): **I** Influence/afterlife · **C** Contestedness ·
**P** Popularity/familiarity (within the Latter Day Saint world and beyond) · **H** Harmful
reception · **A** Historical attestation. Each 0–5, scored independently, evidence-anchored,
refreshable.

**A note on the Attestation (A) axis here.** Per `R0`, the A axis encodes the
*academic-historical* assessment of whether an event is externally attested — it
"rates the event," citing evidence or its absence. Because the Book of Mormon's
ancient-American setting **as a whole** lacks external archaeological, genetic, or
linguistic corroboration and is contradicted on specific points (mainstream consensus;
[see Origins]), units that assert **datable ancient events** score **A1** (the same
grade `R1` gives a global flood), while **doctrinal discourses, sermons, visions,
allegory, quoted scripture, and editorial notes** — which make no testable historical
claim — score **A0**. The A score is *not* a verdict on theological truth: the **LDS
faith position** (the events are historical) and the **apologetic** responses are
recorded in the Origins section and the entry bodies, not folded into A. This keeps the
three-voices rule intact while letting A do its defined job.

**A note on the three voices.** Per the project's neutrality rule, every contested
claim keeps three things distinct: (a) **LDS faith claims** (attributed to the Church
or Joseph Smith), (b) **mainstream academic/scientific consensus** (attributed to
named scholars or fields), and (c) **LDS apologetic arguments** (attributed to their
proponents — the Maxwell Institute, the Interpreter Foundation, Scripture Central,
John W. Welch, John L. Sorenson, etc.). This file reports these positions; it does
not adjudicate theological truth. The historicity debate is summarized in one place
(below) and cross-referenced from individual entries rather than re-argued each time.

**A note on structure.** The Book of Mormon is mediated through three narrator-authors
— **Nephi**, **Mormon**, and **Moroni** (the framework analyzed in Grant Hardy,
*Understanding the Book of Mormon*, Oxford 2010). Two record-streams interleave: the
**small plates of Nephi** (1 Nephi–Omni, first-person, devotional) and **Mormon's
abridgment of the large plates** (Mosiah onward, third-person, with editorial
intrusions). The book of **Ether** is a further abridgment-within-the-abridgment (by
Moroni) of a far older people, the Jaredites.

## Origins and historicity — the debate in brief

Referenced from the entries below as **[see Origins]**.

**The LDS faith account.** Joseph Smith (1805–1844) taught that an angel named
**Moroni** directed him to a set of **golden plates** inscribed in "reformed Egyptian"
buried near Palmyra, New York; obtained in 1827 and translated "by the gift and power
of God" (1828–1829) using the **Urim and Thummim** (the "interpreters") and a **seer
stone**. The Church's 2013 Gospel Topics essay *Book of Mormon Translation*
acknowledges Smith often placed the seer stone in a hat to exclude light. The
testimonies of the **Three Witnesses** (Oliver Cowdery, David Whitmer, Martin Harris)
and **Eight Witnesses** are printed in every edition. First published **March 1830**
in Palmyra (5,000 copies, on Egbert B. Grandin's press). Smith called it "the most
correct of any book on earth and the keystone of our religion."

**The book's internal claims.** A record of Israelite-descended peoples in the
ancient Americas — **Nephites** and **Lamanites**, descended from the family of
**Lehi**, who left Jerusalem c. 600 BCE — plus the earlier **Jaredites** (from the
time of the Tower of Babel), running to c. 421 CE, abridged chiefly by Mormon.

**Mainstream academic/scientific consensus.** Treated as a **19th-century work**
reflecting its American environment. Principal arguments: **anachronisms** (horses,
cattle, sheep, swine, wheat, barley, steel, iron, chariots, silk) lacking
pre-Columbian support; **no archaeological, linguistic, or epigraphic corroboration**
of Nephite/Jaredite peoples or "reformed Egyptian"; **genetic studies** showing Native
Americans descend principally from Asian populations (the Church's DNA essay responds
that the text may describe a small group whose markers could be undetectable, and the
2006 edition changed the introduction's "principal ancestors" to "**among** the
ancestors"); and parallels to 19th-century concerns (Native American origins debates,
anti-universalism, infant-baptism controversy, revivalism). Ethan Smith's *View of
the Hebrews* (1823) is often cited as an environmental parallel; the LDS General
Authority **B. H. Roberts** privately catalogued parallels he found troubling, though
he did not publicly renounce his belief.

**The KJV-dependence question.** The large quoted blocks (Isaiah, the Sermon on the
Mount, Malachi) follow the 1769 **King James Version**, including its *italicized*
supplied words — which Stan Larson and David P. Wright argue indicates dependence on
the printed English Bible. The Church's own footnote to 2 Nephi 12 notes that the
Book of Mormon Isaiah differs from the KJV in more than half of the 433 quoted Isaiah
verses while ~200 match the KJV wording. Much of Isaiah 40–66 ("Deutero-/Trito-Isaiah")
is dated by most critical scholars **after 600 BCE**, which (on that dating) would
post-date Lehi's departure — a recurring crux flagged at the relevant entries.

**LDS apologetic responses (attributed as apologetics).** FARMS (now the **Maxwell
Institute**), the **Interpreter Foundation**, and **Scripture Central** argue for
**limited-geography** (usually Mesoamerican) models (John L. Sorenson, *Mormon's
Codex*); **chiasmus** and Hebrew literary forms (John W. Welch on **Alma 36** —
critics like Earl Wunderli counter that the proposed structure omits much of the
chapter); Hebraisms and Near Eastern names; and **Arabian "Lehi's trail"** geography.

**Positions within the movement.** The **LDS Church** (Salt Lake City) holds
historicity as essential. The **Community of Christ** (formerly RLDS; ~250,000
members) affirms it as scripture but takes **no official position on historicity**
and does not make belief in it a test of fellowship; many members read it
non-historically. Versification also differs between the LDS and Community of Christ
editions; this file uses standard **LDS chapter:verse**.

# 1 NEPHI
*Narrator:* Nephi (first person). *Setting:* Jerusalem → the Arabian wilderness →
ocean crossing → the promised land, c. 600–589 BCE. *Genre:* prophetic migration memoir.

> **Lehi's Call and the Flight from Jerusalem** · `1 Ne 1–2` · *call-vision + migration narrative*
> The prophet Lehi sees a pillar of fire and a heavenly book, prophesies Jerusalem's fall and a coming Messiah, is threatened, and leads his family into the wilderness. His sons Laman and Lemuel murmur; Nephi prays and believes.
> **Related:** Jeremiah and the fall of Jerusalem (`→R1` 2 Kings 24–25; Jeremiah); the call echoes Isaiah 6 and Ezekiel 1–2; the departure is framed as an exodus. **Keywords:** `call` `vision` `exodus-deliverance` `Jerusalem` `Lehi` `Messiah` `murmuring`. **Score:** `I3 · C2 · P4 · H0 · A1`

> **Lehi's Vision of the Tree of Life** · `1 Ne 8` · *dream-vision / allegory*
> Lehi dreams of a tree bearing fruit "desirable to make one happy" (the love of God), reached by holding to an **iron rod** along a strait path through "mists of darkness," past a "great and spacious building" of mockers and a river of filthiness. A central, much-taught LDS image.
> **Related:** the tree of life (`→R1` Gen 2–3; Prov 3:18; Rev 22); expanded and interpreted in Nephi's vision below. **Keywords:** `dream-revelation` `tree-of-life` `iron-rod` `love-of-God` `pride` `allegory`. **Score:** `I4 · C2 · P5 · H0 · A0`

> **Obtaining the Brass Plates; the Killing of Laban** · `1 Ne 3–4` · *moral narrative*
> Sent back to Jerusalem for the brass plates (scripture and genealogy), Nephi finds Laban drunk and, prompted by the Spirit ("it is better that one man should perish than that a nation should dwindle... in unbelief," 4:13), kills him, takes the plates, and brings away Laban's servant Zoram. One of the book's hardest ethical episodes.
> **Related:** the brass plates are said to contain Isaiah and the books of Moses (`→R1`/`→R2`); compared by readers to David and Goliath and to other divinely-sanctioned killings. **Keywords:** `narrative` `divine-command` `killing` `Laban` `brass-plates` `scripture-preservation`. **Score:** `I3 · C4 · P4 · H1 · A1`
> *(C4: the ethics of a divinely-commanded killing are debated inside and outside the tradition.)*

> **Nephi's Apocalyptic Vision** · `1 Ne 11–14` · *vision / prophecy*
> Nephi asks to see his father's dream and is shown Christ's birth and ministry, the future of his seed, the European arrival in the Americas, the coming-forth of the Bible, the "great and abominable church," and the latter days. Introduces the thesis that "plain and precious things" were removed from the Bible.
> **Related:** apocalyptic form (`→R1` Revelation); the "plain and precious things" thesis underwrites the whole book's self-understanding. **Keywords:** `apocalyptic` `vision` `Christology` `Gentiles` `restoration` `plain-and-precious`. **Score:** `I3 · C3 · P3 · H0 · A0`

> **The Broken Bow** · `1 Ne 16` · *wilderness narrative*
> Nephi's fine steel bow breaks; the families hunger and even Lehi murmurs; Nephi makes a wooden bow and, directed by the **Liahona** (a divine compass), finds game. A staple lesson on faith amid setback.
> **Related:** the murmuring-and-provision pattern echoes the wilderness manna narratives (`→R1` Exodus 16; Numbers 11). **Keywords:** `wilderness` `broken-bow` `Liahona` `murmuring` `faith` `providence`. **Score:** `I2 · C2 · P4 · H0 · A1`
> *(C2: "steel" bow c. 600 BCE is among the cited anachronisms — [see Origins].)*

> **Building the Ship; Crossing the Ocean** · `1 Ne 17–18` · *deliverance narrative*
> At a coastal land they call Bountiful, Nephi is shown how to build a ship; his brothers bind him, a storm nearly sinks them, and on his release the sea calms and they reach the promised land. Nephi rehearses the Exodus (17:23–43) to justify the journey.
> **Related:** explicit exodus typology (`→R1` Exodus 14); Noah's-ark resonance (`→R1` Gen 6–9; `→R5` ANE flood/ark traditions). **Keywords:** `ship` `ocean-crossing` `exodus-deliverance` `promised-land` `rebellion` `Nephi`. **Score:** `I3 · C2 · P4 · H0 · A1`

# 2 NEPHI
*Narrator:* Nephi. *Setting:* the promised land, c. 588–545 BCE. *Genre:* doctrinal
testament, prophecy, large quoted-Isaiah block.

> **Lehi's Final Blessings; the Doctrine of the Fall** · `2 Ne 1–3` (esp. ch. 2) · *doctrinal discourse*
> The dying Lehi blesses his children and teaches that there must be "opposition in all things" (2:11), that "Adam fell that men might be; and men are, that they might have joy" (2:25) — a notably **positive** reading of the Fall — and prophesies a latter-day seer named Joseph (ch. 3).
> **Related:** Genesis 2–3 and Romans 5 (`→R1`), but reversing the traditional sense of the Fall toward agency and joy — a distinctive LDS theology. **Keywords:** `fall` `agency` `opposition` `atonement` `Adam` `joy` `Joseph-seer`. **Score:** `I4 · C3 · P4 · H0 · A0`

> **The Division into Nephites and Lamanites; the "Skin of Blackness"** · `2 Ne 5` · *etiological narrative + curse text*
> After Lehi's death the family splits: Nephi's followers (Nephites) separate from Laman's (Lamanites). The text states the Lord caused "a skin of blackness to come upon" the Lamanites so they would "not be enticing" (5:21). This etiology frames the millennium-long conflict and is the first of the book's racial-curse passages.
> **Related:** the parallel curse texts (Jacob 3:5–9; Alma 3; 3 Ne 2:15; `1 Ne 12:23`; Mormon 5:15); reception history alongside the biblical "mark of Cain"/"curse of Ham" (`→R1` Gen 4; 9). **Keywords:** `etiology` `Nephites` `Lamanites` `fraternal-conflict` `curse` `skin-of-blackness` `race`. **Score:** `I4 · C5 · P3 · H4 · A1`
> *(H4: these passages, with statements by Brigham Young and others, were used to support the LDS priesthood/temple ban on members of Black African descent, 1852–1978. The Church's 2013 essay "Race and the Priesthood" disavows "the theories advanced in the past that black skin is a sign of divine disfavor or curse" and "unequivocally condemn[s] all racism, past and present." Reported as documented harmful reception; framed and rejected, never endorsed.)*

> **The Large Isaiah Block** · `2 Ne 12–24` (= Isaiah 2–14) · *quoted prophecy*
> Nephi quotes roughly thirteen consecutive chapters of Isaiah, nearly verbatim from the KJV, as a second witness of the gathering of Israel and the Messiah.
> **Related:** `→R1` Isaiah 2–14; the **KJV-italics** dependence question and the **Deutero-Isaiah dating** crux ([see Origins]). **Keywords:** `quotation` `Isaiah` `KJV-dependence` `scattering-gathering` `messianic-hope`. **Score:** `I2 · C5 · P2 · H0 · A0`

> **Nephi's Commentary and the Doctrine of Christ** · `2 Ne 25–33` · *prophetic commentary*
> Nephi interprets Isaiah ("I glory in plainness," 33:6), foretells a future record sealed and brought forth (ch. 27, echoing Isaiah 29), and lays out the "doctrine of Christ" — faith, repentance, baptism, the Holy Ghost (ch. 31).
> **Related:** Isaiah 29 (`→R1`); the baptism formula echoes Matthew 3 / John's baptism (`→R1`). **Keywords:** `commentary` `doctrine-of-Christ` `baptism` `sealed-book` `plainness`. **Score:** `I3 · C3 · P3 · H0 · A0`

# JACOB
*Narrator:* Jacob (Nephi's brother). *Setting:* land of Nephi, c. 544–421 BCE.
*Genre:* temple sermon, allegory, anti-Christ dialogue.

> **Jacob's Temple Sermon** · `Jacob 1–3` · *sermon*
> Jacob preaches against pride and the love of riches, and condemns unauthorized polygamy — "David and Solomon... had many wives... which thing was abominable" (2:24) — while noting the Lamanites' marital fidelity puts the Nephites to shame.
> **Related:** the conditional clause at 2:30 ("if I will... raise up seed... I will command") later figures in LDS plural-marriage debates; prophetic social critique echoes Amos (`→R1`). **Keywords:** `temple-sermon` `pride` `riches` `chastity` `polygamy`. **Score:** `I3 · C4 · P3 · H1 · A0`

> **The Allegory of the Olive Tree (Zenos)** · `Jacob 5` (with 6) · *extended allegory*
> The book's longest chapter, attributed to an otherwise-unknown prophet **Zenos**: the house of Israel is a tame olive tree that the Lord of the vineyard repeatedly grafts, prunes, transplants, and labors to preserve across the ages — an allegory of scattering and gathering.
> **Related:** Paul's olive-tree image (`→R1` Romans 11:16–24); Isaiah 5:1–7 (the vineyard); Jeremiah 11:16; Hosea 14:6. Dependence on Romans vs. a shared/lost source is debated. **Keywords:** `allegory` `olive-tree` `Zenos` `scattering-gathering` `grafting` `Israel`. **Score:** `I3 · C3 · P3 · H0 · A0`

> **Sherem the Anti-Christ** · `Jacob 7` · *confrontation narrative*
> Sherem disputes the doctrine of Christ, demands a sign, is struck down, and confesses the truth before dying — the first of the book's three "anti-Christ" type-scenes.
> **Related:** Nehor (Alma 1) and Korihor (Alma 30) complete the type. **Keywords:** `anti-Christ` `Sherem` `sign-seeking` `apologetics`. **Score:** `I2 · C2 · P3 · H0 · A1`

# ENOS · JAROM · OMNI
*Narrators:* Enos, Jarom, and five successive keepers (Omni). *Setting:* c. 544–130 BCE.
*Genre:* brief personal records / transition. — **grouped, with one unit flagged**

> **Enos's Prayer** · `Enos 1` · *conversion / prayer narrative*
> Enos prays through the day and into the night in the forest until his sins are forgiven, then prays for the Nephites, for the Lamanites, and for the preservation of the records.
> **Related:** the all-night wrestle echoes Jacob at Peniel (`→R1` Gen 32). **Keywords:** `prayer` `forgiveness` `wrestle` `records` `conversion`. **Score:** `I2 · C1 · P4 · H0 · A1`

> **Jarom, Omni, and the Discovery of Zarahemla** · `Jarom 1`; `Omni 1` · *chronicle* — **grouped**
> Brief notices carry the record forward; Mosiah discovers the people of **Zarahemla** (Mulekites, said to descend from a son of King Zedekiah), and the last Jaredite, **Coriantumr**, is mentioned; Amaleki hands the small plates to King Benjamin.
> **Related:** the Mulekite link to Zedekiah (`→R1` Jeremiah 52:10); Coriantumr ties forward to Ether. **Keywords:** `Mulekites` `Zarahemla` `Coriantumr` `record-transmission`. **Score:** `I2 · C2 · P1 · H0 · A1`

# WORDS OF MORMON
*Narrator:* Mormon (editorial). *Setting:* written c. 385 CE. *Genre:* editorial bridge.

> **Mormon's Bridge** · `W of M 1` · *editorial note*
> Mormon explains that he appends the small plates to his abridgment "for a wise purpose" known to the Lord — the narrative device that absorbs the loss of the 116-page manuscript.
> **Related:** the lost-manuscript episode (LDS Doctrine & Covenants 10). **Keywords:** `editorial` `small-plates` `wise-purpose`. **Score:** `I2 · C3 · P1 · H0 · A0`

# MOSIAH
*Narrator:* Mormon (abridging). *Setting:* Zarahemla and the land of Nephi, c. 130–91 BCE.
*Genre:* kingship narrative, coronation sermon, conversion, deliverance.

> **King Benjamin's Address** · `Mosiah 2–5` · *coronation sermon*
> From a tower by the temple, the aged King Benjamin teaches that "when ye are in the service of your fellow beings ye are only in the service of your God" (2:17) and that "the natural man is an enemy to God... unless he yields to the enticings of the Holy Spirit" (3:19); the people covenant to take upon them the name of Christ. One of the most-cited and most-loved passages in the book.
> **Related:** ethics paralleling the Sermon on the Mount (`→R1` Matthew 5–7); a proposed Israelite festival (Tabernacles) setting; a noted chiasmus at 5:10–12. **Keywords:** `coronation-sermon` `service` `natural-man` `covenant` `atonement` `King-Benjamin`. **Score:** `I4 · C2 · P5 · H0 · A0`

> **Abinadi Before King Noah; His Martyrdom** · `Mosiah 11–17` · *prophetic trial / martyrdom*
> The prophet Abinadi confronts the wicked King Noah's court, quotes Isaiah 53 in full, expounds the suffering Messiah, refuses to recant, and is burned to death. The young priest **Alma** secretly believes and writes his words down — the hinge to the whole Alma tradition.
> **Related:** Isaiah 52:7–10 and 53 (`→R1`); the suffering-servant typology; the prophet-vs-king pattern of Elijah and Ahab (`→R1` 1 Kings); martyrdom echoes Stephen (`→R1` Acts 7). **Keywords:** `martyrdom` `Abinadi` `King-Noah` `Isaiah-53` `suffering-servant` `fire`. **Score:** `I3 · C3 · P4 · H0 · A1`

> **Alma the Elder and the Waters of Mormon** · `Mosiah 18` · *covenant / church-founding*
> Fleeing Noah, Alma gathers believers at the waters of Mormon, teaches them to "bear one another's burdens" and "mourn with those that mourn," baptizes them, and organizes the church.
> **Related:** baptism imagery (`→R1` Matthew 3); the community-of-burden-bearing echoes Acts; baptism centuries BCE is debated ([see Origins]). **Keywords:** `baptism` `waters-of-Mormon` `church-founding` `covenant` `Alma-elder`. **Score:** `I3 · C3 · P4 · H0 · A1`

> **Deliverance of the Peoples of Limhi and Alma** · `Mosiah 19–24` · *deliverance narrative* — **grouped**
> Two Nephite groups fall under Lamanite bondage and are miraculously delivered; God promises to "ease the burdens" on Alma's people (24:14–15).
> **Related:** Exodus deliverance typology (`→R1` Exodus). **Keywords:** `bondage` `deliverance` `exodus-deliverance` `Limhi`. **Score:** `I2 · C2 · P3 · H0 · A1`

> **The Reign of the Judges Established** · `Mosiah 29` · *political / legal discourse*
> King Mosiah replaces the monarchy with a system of judges chosen "by the voice of the people," warning against the dangers of an unrighteous king.
> **Related:** the anti-monarchy warning echoes 1 Samuel 8 (`→R1`); read by critics as 19th-century American republicanism ([see Origins]). **Keywords:** `judges` `voice-of-the-people` `anti-monarchy` `law`. **Score:** `I3 · C3 · P3 · H0 · A1`

# ALMA
*Narrator:* Mormon (abridging Alma, Ammon, and Helaman records). *Setting:* c. 91–52 BCE.
*Genre:* the longest book — conversion, missionary saga, sermon, and war chronicle.

> **The Conversion of Alma the Younger and the Sons of Mosiah** · `Mosiah 27` / `Alma 36` · *conversion narrative*
> An angel halts Alma the Younger and his companions as they persecute the church; struck dumb, Alma passes through "the pains of a damned soul," is born again, and becomes the great Nephite missionary. The retelling in **Alma 36** is the showcase example of proposed **chiasmus**.
> **Related:** Paul's Damascus-road conversion (`→R1` Acts 9); John W. Welch's chiasmus argument vs. Earl Wunderli's critique ([see Origins]). **Keywords:** `conversion` `Alma-younger` `sons-of-Mosiah` `chiasmus` `rebirth`. **Score:** `I4 · C3 · P5 · H0 · A1`

> **Nehor and Priestcraft** · `Alma 1` · *confrontation narrative*
> Nehor introduces "priestcraft" — preaching for money and teaching universal salvation — kills the aged Gideon, and is executed; his "order" recurs as a destabilizing faction.
> **Related:** 19th-century anti-universalism ([see Origins]); Simon Magus (`→R1` Acts 8). **Keywords:** `Nehor` `priestcraft` `universalism` `anti-Christ`. **Score:** `I2 · C2 · P3 · H0 · A1`

> **Alma's Sermon at Zarahemla** · `Alma 5` · *sermon*
> Alma presses some fifty searching questions on personal conversion — "have ye experienced this mighty change in your hearts?" — and "received his image in your countenances?"
> **Related:** the new-birth theme (`→R1` John 3). **Keywords:** `born-again` `self-examination` `mighty-change` `Alma-5`. **Score:** `I3 · C1 · P4 · H0 · A0`

> **Ammon and King Lamoni** · `Alma 17–20` · *missionary narrative*
> Ammon, serving as King Lamoni's herdsman, defends the king's flocks at the waters of Sebus by cutting off the raiders' arms; astonished, Lamoni and his household are converted. A favorite missionary-hero tale.
> **Related:** part of the sons-of-Mosiah mission to the Lamanites. **Keywords:** `Ammon` `Lamoni` `missionary` `flocks` `conversion`. **Score:** `I3 · C2 · P5 · H0 · A1`

> **The Anti-Nephi-Lehies Bury Their Weapons** · `Alma 23–24` · *pacifism / martyrdom*
> Converted Lamanites covenant never again to shed blood, bury their swords deep in the earth, and allow 1,005 of their number to be slain without resistance; many of the attackers, stricken, convert on the spot.
> **Related:** nonviolence (`→R1` Matthew 5:39); their sons later become the stripling warriors (Helaman). Some editions/readers note "curse-lifting" language about the converts. **Keywords:** `pacifism` `Anti-Nephi-Lehies` `buried-weapons` `martyrdom` `covenant`. **Score:** `I3 · C3 · P5 · H1 · A1`

> **Korihor the Anti-Christ** · `Alma 30` · *apologetic dialogue*
> Korihor preaches that there is no God, no Christ, and no sin — that "every man fared in this life according to the management of the creature." Debating Alma, he demands a sign, is struck dumb, and is later trampled to death.
> **Related:** completes the anti-Christ type (Sherem; Nehor); "the fool hath said... there is no God" (`→R1` Psalm 14). Debated as a caricature of skepticism. **Keywords:** `Korihor` `anti-Christ` `atheism` `sign-seeking` `apologetics`. **Score:** `I3 · C3 · P4 · H1 · A1`

> **The Zoramites and the Rameumptom** · `Alma 31` · *satire / apostasy narrative*
> The apostate Zoramites pray a fixed, weekly prayer from atop a high stand (the **Rameumptom**), thanking God that they are a chosen, "elect" people while others are doomed.
> **Related:** the self-righteous prayer recalls Luke 18 (the Pharisee and the publican, `→R1`). **Keywords:** `Zoramites` `Rameumptom` `pride` `set-prayer` `election`. **Score:** `I2 · C2 · P4 · H0 · A1`

> **Alma's Sermon on Faith and the Seed** · `Alma 32` (with 33–34) · *doctrinal discourse*
> To the poor and humble Zoramites, Alma compares **faith to a seed**: plant the word, and if it is good it will swell and sprout and grow — "faith is not to have a perfect knowledge"; it is hope in things "not seen, which are true." Amulek follows with the doctrine of the "great and last sacrifice."
> **Related:** faith as substance of things hoped for (`→R1` Hebrews 11:1); the sower and the mustard seed (`→R1` Mark 4; Matthew 13). **Keywords:** `faith` `seed` `experiment` `hope` `atonement` `Alma-32` `Zoramites`. **Score:** `I4 · C1 · P5 · H0 · A0`

> **The War Chapters: Captain Moroni and the Title of Liberty** · `Alma 43–63` (Title of Liberty, `Alma 46`) · *war narrative* — **grouped, with one unit flagged**
> A long, detailed war chronicle. **Captain Moroni** tears his coat and writes on it the **title of liberty** — "In memory of our God, our religion, and freedom, and our peace, our wives, and our children" — rallies the Nephites, fortifies cities, and defeats the ambitious dissenter Amalickiah. The vocabulary of "freedom" and "liberty" is dense here.
> **Related:** Old Testament holy-war narratives (`→R1` Joshua); the rent-garment sign (`→R1` 1 Samuel 15:27–28); read as patriotic typology in American LDS culture. **Keywords:** `war` `Captain-Moroni` `title-of-liberty` `freedom` `dissent` `Amalickiah`. **Score:** `I3 · C3 · P4 · H1 · A1`

# HELAMAN
*Narrator:* Mormon (abridging). *Setting:* c. 52–1 BCE. *Genre:* political-decline narrative, prophecy.

> **The Two Thousand Stripling Warriors** · `Alma 53, 56–58` (Helaman's letter) · *war / faith narrative*
> The young sons of the pacifist Ammonites — who had not taken the oath against bloodshed — take up arms under **Helaman**; though all are wounded, **not one is slain** in their first engagement, which they credit to the faith their mothers taught them ("we do not doubt our mothers knew it," 56:48).
> **Related:** the few-against-many deliverance recalls Gideon (`→R1` Judges 7). **Keywords:** `stripling-warriors` `faith` `mothers` `Helaman` `deliverance`. **Score:** `I3 · C1 · P5 · H0 · A1`

> **The Gadianton Robbers and the Pride Cycle** · `Hel 1–11` · *political-decline narrative* — **grouped**
> A secret oath-bound band, the **Gadianton robbers**, infiltrates Nephite government; the society spirals through accelerating "pride cycles" of prosperity, pride, downfall, and repentance. Nephi son of Helaman receives the sealing power.
> **Related:** "secret combinations" (Ether 8) — read by some critics as reflecting 19th-century anti-Masonic anxieties ([see Origins]). **Keywords:** `Gadianton` `secret-combinations` `pride-cycle` `corruption`. **Score:** `I3 · C3 · P3 · H0 · A1`

> **Samuel the Lamanite on the Wall** · `Hel 13–16` · *prophecy*
> Cast out of Zarahemla, the Lamanite prophet **Samuel** prophesies from the city wall: in five years the Messiah will be born, marked by a night without darkness and a new star; at his death there will be three days of darkness with great storms and earthquakes. Arrows and stones cannot touch him.
> **Related:** the star (`→R1` Matthew 2); the crucifixion darkness (`→R1` Matthew 27:45–51); fulfilled in 3 Nephi 1 and 8. A rare case of a Lamanite prophet to the Nephites. **Keywords:** `Samuel-the-Lamanite` `wall` `signs` `Lamanite-prophet` `messianic-hope`. **Score:** `I3 · C2 · P4 · H0 · A1`

# 3 NEPHI
*Narrator:* Mormon (abridging). *Setting:* c. 1–35 CE. *Genre:* the doctrinal and dramatic climax of the book.

> **The Signs of Christ's Birth** · `3 Ne 1` · *sign-fulfillment narrative*
> Unbelievers set a day to execute the believers unless Samuel's sign appears; that night there is no darkness, and a new star arises, beginning a new reckoning of time.
> **Related:** fulfills Helaman 14; the Nativity star (`→R1` Luke 2; Matthew 2). **Keywords:** `birth-sign` `no-darkness` `new-star` `deliverance`. **Score:** `I3 · C2 · P4 · H0 · A1`

> **The Signs of Christ's Death; the Voice in the Darkness** · `3 Ne 8–10` · *cataclysm narrative*
> Storms, earthquakes, fire, and tempest destroy and sink cities; three days of thick darkness follow, during which the voice of the as-yet-unseen Christ names the destroyed cities, mourns the people, and calls survivors to repentance.
> **Related:** the crucifixion cataclysm (`→R1` Matthew 27:45–54); the plague of darkness (`→R1` Exodus 10); the prophecy of Zenos cited at 1 Nephi 19:10. **Keywords:** `death-signs` `darkness` `cataclysm` `voice-of-Christ` `repentance`. **Score:** `I3 · C3 · P4 · H0 · A1`

> **The Appearance of the Resurrected Jesus Christ** · `3 Ne 11` · *theophany*
> The Father's voice introduces the Son; the resurrected **Jesus Christ descends** at the temple in Bountiful; the whole multitude come forward **one by one** to feel the wounds in his hands, feet, and side; he calls twelve disciples, gives authority to baptize, and forbids contention. The book's climactic scene.
> **Related:** Thomas touching the wounds, here extended to the whole multitude (`→R1` John 20:24–29); the Great Commission (`→R1` Matthew 28). **Keywords:** `theophany` `resurrected-Christ` `Bountiful` `twelve-disciples` `wounds`. **Score:** `I5 · C3 · P5 · H0 · A1`

> **The Sermon at the Temple (the Sermon on the Mount)** · `3 Ne 12–14` (= Matthew 5–7) · *sermon*
> Jesus delivers to the Nephites a version of the **Sermon on the Mount**, with the Beatitudes adapted ("blessed are they who shall believe in your words... they shall be filled with the Holy Ghost"); he declares the law of Moses fulfilled in him and invites his hearers to "be perfect even as I, or your Father."
> **Related:** Matthew 5–7 (`→R1`), with which it largely agrees verbatim against the KJV; on the **Synoptic "Q"/Sermon-source** question and the KJV-italics issue, a literal pre-resurrection delivery is anachronistic to critics ([see Origins]). **Keywords:** `Sermon-on-the-Mount` `Beatitudes` `KJV-dependence` `law-fulfilled` `perfection`. **Score:** `I3 · C5 · P4 · H0 · A0`

> **Christ's Ministry, Doctrine, and Departure** · `3 Ne 15–28` · *ministry / discourse* — **grouped, with flagged unit**
> Jesus names the Nephites the "**other sheep**" of John 10:16, institutes the sacrament, quotes **Isaiah 54** and **Malachi 3–4** (3 Ne 22; 24–25), prophesies the latter-day gathering, heals the sick, blesses the children as angels descend, and grants three disciples to tarry until his return (the "**Three Nephites**").
> **Related:** "other sheep" (`→R1` John 10:16); Isaiah 52, 54 and Malachi 3–4 (`→R1`); Malachi (composed c. 5th c. BCE) post-dates Lehi, a flagged crux ([see Origins]). **Keywords:** `other-sheep` `sacrament` `Isaiah` `Malachi` `gathering` `Three-Nephites`. **Score:** `I3 · C4 · P4 · H0 · A1`

# 4 NEPHI
*Narrator:* Mormon. *Setting:* c. 35–321 CE. *Genre:* utopian chronicle.

> **The Two Hundred Years of Peace** · `4 Ne 1` · *utopian / decline narrative*
> After Christ's visit, all the people are converted; there are "no contentions," "all things common among them," "neither rich nor poor," and "no... -ites" — a unified, righteous society lasting roughly two centuries before pride, costly apparel, class divisions, revived tribal labels, and renewed Gadianton bands return.
> **Related:** the community of goods (`→R1` Acts 2:44–45; 4:32); the LDS ideal of Zion (the city of Enoch). **Keywords:** `utopia` `all-things-common` `Zion` `decline` `pride`. **Score:** `I3 · C1 · P3 · H0 · A1`

# MORMON
*Narrator:* Mormon, then Moroni. *Setting:* c. 322–421 CE. *Genre:* military memoir, lament, final witness.

> **Mormon's Account of the Final Wars** · `Mormon 1–6` · *war memoir / lament*
> Mormon, a Nephite general from boyhood, chronicles the relentless final wars, hides the sacred records in the **hill Cumorah**, and at the last battle (c. 385 CE) sees twenty-three commanders, each leading ten thousand, fall with their armies; wounded, he laments over the slain "fair ones."
> **Related:** the lament form (`→R1` Lamentations); the Jaredites perished at the same hill (Ramah/Cumorah; see Ether). **Keywords:** `Mormon` `Cumorah` `final-war` `lament` `destruction`. **Score:** `I3 · C3 · P3 · H0 · A1`

> **Moroni's Witness on the Latter Days** · `Mormon 7–9` · *prophetic address*
> Moroni finishes his father's book, addresses both the future Lamanite remnant and the latter-day Gentile reader, defends the record against future skeptics, and testifies that miracles have not ceased.
> **Related:** anticipates Moroni 10. **Keywords:** `Moroni` `latter-day` `defense` `miracles` `witness`. **Score:** `I3 · C3 · P3 · H0 · A0`

# ETHER
*Narrator:* Moroni (abridging the 24 Jaredite plates). *Setting:* from the Tower of
Babel (c. 2200 BCE) to before Lehi's arrival. *Genre:* primeval migration epic, vision.

> **The Jaredites Leave the Tower of Babel** · `Ether 1–2` · *migration narrative*
> At the confounding of languages, the **brother of Jared** prays that his people's language be spared; led by the Lord, they journey and build barges to cross the sea to a promised land.
> **Related:** the Tower of Babel (`→R1` Gen 11); a second, far older migration paralleling Lehi's. **Keywords:** `Jaredites` `Babel` `brother-of-Jared` `barges` `migration`. **Score:** `I2 · C3 · P3 · H0 · A1`

> **The Brother of Jared's Vision; the Sixteen Shining Stones** · `Ether 3` (with 2:22–25; 4; 6) · *theophany / vision*
> To light the windowless barges, the brother of Jared molds sixteen small transparent stones and asks the Lord to touch them. Seeing the Lord's finger "as the finger of a man," his faith is so great that he is brought through the veil and beholds the **premortal Christ** ("Behold, I am Jesus Christ... the Father and the Son"), who shows him all the inhabitants of the earth. The account and two interpreter stones are **sealed up**.
> **Related:** Moses denied the full sight of God's face (`→R1` Exodus 33); shining-stone and ark-of-light legends (`→R5`); the interpreter stones connect to the Urim and Thummim. **Keywords:** `theophany` `brother-of-Jared` `sixteen-stones` `premortal-Christ` `veil` `sealed-record`. **Score:** `I3 · C3 · P4 · H0 · A0`

> **The Jaredite Civilization and the War of Extinction** · `Ether 8–15` (esp. 13–15) · *dynastic chronicle / war of annihilation* — **grouped**
> After cycles of kings, **secret combinations**, and prophets, the prophet Ether warns **Coriantumr** that all his people will be destroyed unless they repent. The final war with **Shiz** exterminates the nation — nearly two million slain — until Coriantumr beheads Shiz and is left the sole survivor, later found by the people of Zarahemla.
> **Related:** the deliberate parallel to the Nephite extinction (Mormon 6); secret combinations (anti-Masonic reading, [see Origins]); Ramah = Cumorah. **Keywords:** `Coriantumr` `Shiz` `extinction` `secret-combinations` `sole-survivor`. **Score:** `I3 · C3 · P3 · H0 · A1`

# MORONI
*Narrator:* Moroni. *Setting:* c. 401–421 CE. *Genre:* ecclesiastical handbook, epistles, closing testimony.

> **Ordinances, Epistles, and Charity** · `Moroni 1–9` · *church-order + letters* — **grouped**
> The manner of conferring the priesthood, the sacramental prayers, and church practice; two letters of Mormon, including the discourse on **faith, hope, and charity** — "charity is the pure love of Christ" (7:47) — and a wrenching letter on the final war's atrocities.
> **Related:** the love chapter (`→R1` 1 Corinthians 13); the pastoral epistles; "ask in faith" (`→R1` James 1). **Keywords:** `charity` `sacrament` `priesthood` `epistles` `church-order`. **Score:** `I3 · C1 · P4 · H0 · A0`

> **Moroni's Promise** · `Moroni 10:3–5` · *closing exhortation*
> Moroni's farewell invites readers to ponder the record and then "ask God, the Eternal Father, in the name of Christ, if these things are not true," promising that to one who asks "with a sincere heart, with real intent, having faith in Christ," the truth will be made known "by the power of the Holy Ghost." The single most-quoted passage in LDS missionary use.
> **Related:** "ask... it shall be given" (`→R1` James 1:5; Matthew 7:7); "if any man will do his will, he shall know" (`→R1` John 7:17). **Keywords:** `Moronis-promise` `prayer` `testimony` `Holy-Ghost` `conversion`. **Score:** `I4 · C3 · P5 · H0 · A0`

## Reception, influence, and harm (scoring notes)

**Influence.** Foundational scripture for the entire Latter Day Saint movement; the
LDS Church reported worldwide membership of **17,509,781 as of December 31, 2024**.
The book anchors a worldwide missionary program and a Church-wide annual study cycle.
Cultural-reception items: the outdoor **Hill Cumorah Pageant** (Palmyra, NY, 1937–2019,
since discontinued) and the satirical Broadway musical ***The Book of Mormon*** (Parker,
Stone & Lopez, 2011), which won nine Tony Awards including Best Musical — and which
concerns modern missionaries, not the book's contents.

**Popularity / familiarity.** Among the most-distributed books in modern history:
**5,000 copies** in the 1830 first edition; the Church reports surpassing **200 million
copies** (announced December 2023), in 113 languages.

**Contestedness.** Beyond historicity ([see Origins]): the text has been revised across
editions — grammatical and stylistic changes from 1830 on; the 2006 introduction change
from "principal ancestors" to "among the ancestors"; and **2 Nephi 30:6**, changed from
"**white** and delightsome" to "**pure** and delightsome" (a change Joseph Smith first
made in the 1840 edition, lost in the line descending from the 1837 text, and restored
in 1981). Critics link the timing to civil-rights sensitivities; LDS textual scholars
(Royal Skousen) emphasize it as Smith's own 1840 emendation.

**Harmful reception (documented; never endorsed).** The racial-curse passages
(2 Ne 5:21; Alma 3; 3 Ne 2:15; Jacob 3:5–9; 1 Ne 12:23; Mormon 5:15), together with
statements by 19th-century leaders, were used to support the LDS **priesthood and
temple ban** on members of Black African descent (**1852–1978**). The Church's 2013
Gospel Topics essay **"Race and the Priesthood"** disavows "the theories advanced in
the past that black skin is a sign of divine disfavor or curse" and "unequivocally
condemn[s] all racism, past and present." Reported here as documented reception/harm
history, clearly framed and rejected — not endorsed.
