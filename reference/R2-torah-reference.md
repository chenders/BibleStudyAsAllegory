# R2 — The Torah (Jewish Framing): Parashot Reference

The Five Books of Moses (the *Chumash*) as received and read in Jewish tradition —
by its Hebrew names, its annual cycle of **54 weekly portions (parashot)**, and its
place in the three-part **Tanakh**. This is the same Pentateuch catalogued in `R1`,
but framed *in its own right*: by the synagogue reading cycle, the Jewish ordering,
and the rabbinic interpretive tradition (midrash, Talmud, the classic commentators),
not as a subset of the Christian Old Testament.

## How the Torah is framed in Judaism

**The Tanakh (תַּנַ״ךְ).** The Hebrew Bible's three divisions, an acronym:
- **Torah** (תּוֹרָה, "Teaching/Instruction") — the Five Books. The most sacred division, read liturgically in full each year.
- **Nevi'im** (נְבִיאִים, "Prophets") — Joshua, Judges, Samuel, Kings ("Former Prophets"); Isaiah, Jeremiah, Ezekiel, and the Twelve ("Latter Prophets").
- **Ketuvim** (כְּתוּבִים, "Writings") — Psalms, Proverbs, Job, the Five Scrolls, Daniel, Ezra–Nehemiah, Chronicles.
  The Tanakh counts **24 books** (vs. the Protestant 39) by treating Samuel, Kings,
  Chronicles, Ezra–Nehemiah, and the Twelve Minor Prophets each as single books. The
  **order differs** from the Christian OT: most importantly, the Tanakh **ends with
  Chronicles** (looking toward return and rebuilding), not with Malachi (which in
  Christian arrangement points forward to the Gospels). The threefold order also
  places Daniel among the Writings, not the Prophets.

**The five books and their Hebrew names** (each from its opening word):

| English | Hebrew | Meaning | Parashot |
|---|---|---|---|
| Genesis | **Bereshit** (בְּרֵאשִׁית) | "In the beginning" | 12 |
| Exodus | **Shemot** (שְׁמוֹת) | "Names" | 11 |
| Leviticus | **Vayikra** (וַיִּקְרָא) | "And He called" | 10 |
| Numbers | **Bamidbar** (בְּמִדְבַּר) | "In the wilderness" | 10 |
| Deuteronomy | **Devarim** (דְּבָרִים) | "Words" | 11 |

**The parashah cycle.** The Torah is divided into **54 parashot** (sing. *parashah*
or *sidra*), read aloud in the synagogue on a one-year cycle that begins and ends on
**Simchat Torah** (immediately re-starting Genesis after finishing Deuteronomy).
Because most years have fewer than 54 Sabbaths, certain adjacent portions are
**doubled** in common years to fit the calendar. Each Shabbat reading is paired with
a **Haftarah** — a thematically linked passage from the Prophets. The division into
parashot is ancient (the Babylonian one-year cycle became standard; an older
triennial cycle existed in the Land of Israel).

**Traditional vs. critical authorship** — as in `R1`: Jewish tradition holds
**Torah mi-Sinai** (the Torah given to Moses at Sinai); academic scholarship reads it
as a composite (the Documentary Hypothesis and its rivals). Orthodox Judaism affirms
Mosaic/divine authorship; Conservative, Reform, and Reconstructionist Judaism
variously engage critical scholarship. This file presents the traditional liturgical
and interpretive frame; `R1` carries the critical-composition notes.

**The interpretive tradition.** Jewish reading proceeds through layers often
abbreviated **PaRDeS**: *Peshat* (plain sense), *Remez* (allusion), *Derash*
(homiletical/midrashic), *Sod* (mystical). Key bodies of interpretation referenced
below: the **Talmud** (Mishnah + Gemara), **midrash** (esp. *Genesis Rabbah*), the
**Targums** (Aramaic translation-paraphrases, e.g. *Pseudo-Jonathan*), and the
medieval commentators **Rashi**, **Ramban** (Nachmanides), and **Ibn Ezra**.

# BERESHIT / Genesis — 12 parashot

> **1. Bereshit** · `Gen 1:1–6:8` · creation, Eden, the Fall, Cain and Abel, the line to Noah.
> *Rabbinic notes:* *Genesis Rabbah* debates what the brothers quarreled over and reads Cain's "mark" as (variously) a horn, leprosy, or a dog; Eve is read as a partner "with the LORD" in creation (4:1). **→R1:** Gen 1–4 entries.

> **2. Noach** · `Gen 6:9–11:32` · the Flood, the rainbow covenant, Noah's drunkenness, Babel.
> *Rabbinic notes:* the **seven Noahide laws** (the moral code binding on all humanity) are derived here; Babel read as a parable of tyranny (Nimrod). **→R1:** Flood, Babel; **→R5:** Gilgamesh, Atrahasis.

> **3. Lech-Lecha** · `Gen 12:1–17:27` · Abram's call, the covenant of the pieces, Hagar/Ishmael, circumcision and the new names.
> *Rabbinic notes:* the *midrash* of Abram smashing his father Terah's idols (not in the text, but famous; cf. `→R3` Quran). **→R1:** Abraham cycle.

> **4. Vayera** · `Gen 18:1–22:24` · the three visitors, Sodom, Isaac's birth, the **Aqedah** (Binding of Isaac).
> *Rabbinic notes:* the Aqedah is read on Rosh Hashanah; midrash explores Isaac's age and willingness and Sarah's death from the shock. **→R1:** Sodom, the Aqedah; **→R3:** Quran 37.

> **5. Chayei Sarah** · `Gen 23:1–25:18` · Sarah's death and burial at Machpelah; Rebekah found for Isaac.

> **6. Toldot** · `Gen 25:19–28:9` · Jacob and Esau; the birthright and the stolen blessing.

> **7. Vayetze** · `Gen 28:10–32:3` · Jacob's ladder; Leah, Rachel, and the children; the flight from Laban.

> **8. Vayishlach** · `Gen 32:4–36:43` · wrestling at Peniel (Jacob → Israel); reconciliation with Esau; Dinah; Rachel's death.

> **9. Vayeshev** · `Gen 37:1–40:23` · Joseph's dreams and sale; Judah and Tamar; Potiphar's wife; the prison dreams.
> *Note:* **→R5:** the Egyptian *Tale of Two Brothers*.

> **10. Miketz** · `Gen 41:1–44:17` · Pharaoh's dreams; Joseph's rise; the brothers in Egypt; the planted cup. *(Often read on Hanukkah.)*

> **11. Vayigash** · `Gen 44:18–47:27` · Judah's plea; Joseph reveals himself; Israel settles in Egypt.

> **12. Vayechi** · `Gen 47:28–50:26` · Jacob blesses his sons (the tribal poem); the deaths of Jacob and Joseph.

# SHEMOT / Exodus — 11 parashot

> **13. Shemot** · `Exod 1:1–6:1` · enslavement; Moses' birth and flight; the burning bush and the divine name. **→R3:** Musa.

> **14. Va'era** · `Exod 6:2–9:35` · the first seven plagues.

> **15. Bo** · `Exod 10:1–13:16` · the last plagues; the **Passover** instituted; the Exodus.
> *Note:* the source of the Seder and the mitzvah of retelling. **→R1:** Passover.

> **16. Beshalach** · `Exod 13:17–17:16` · the **parting of the Sea**; the Song of the Sea (sung daily in liturgy); manna; Amalek. *(Shabbat Shirah, "Sabbath of Song.")*

> **17. Yitro** · `Exod 18:1–20:23` · Jethro's counsel; **Sinai and the Ten Commandments** (the *Aseret ha-Dibrot*).

> **18. Mishpatim** · `Exod 21:1–24:18` · the **Covenant Code** (civil and ritual law); the covenant sealed. **→R5:** Code of Hammurabi.

> **19. Terumah** · `Exod 25:1–27:19` · instructions for the Tabernacle and its furnishings.

> **20. Tetzaveh** · `Exod 27:20–30:10` · the priestly garments and ordination.

> **21. Ki Tisa** · `Exod 30:11–34:35` · the census; the **golden calf**; Moses' intercession and shining face.

> **22. Vayakhel** · `Exod 35:1–38:20` · the Tabernacle built. *(Often doubled with Pekudei.)*

> **23. Pekudei** · `Exod 38:21–40:38` · the Tabernacle completed; the glory fills it.

# VAYIKRA / Leviticus — 10 parashot

> **24. Vayikra** · `Lev 1:1–5:26` · the system of offerings.

> **25. Tzav** · `Lev 6:1–8:36` · further sacrificial law; the priests' ordination.

> **26. Shemini** · `Lev 9:1–11:47` · Nadab and Abihu's death; the **dietary laws** (*kashrut*).

> **27. Tazria** · `Lev 12:1–13:59` · childbirth purity; skin afflictions (*tzaraat*). *(Often doubled with Metzora.)*

> **28. Metzora** · `Lev 14:1–15:33` · purification from *tzaraat* and discharges.

> **29. Acharei Mot** · `Lev 16:1–18:30` · the **Day of Atonement** (Yom Kippur) rite and the scapegoat; forbidden relations. *(Often doubled with Kedoshim.)*

> **30. Kedoshim** · `Lev 19:1–20:27` · the **Holiness Code** core — "love your neighbor as yourself" (19:18); ethical and ritual holiness.

> **31. Emor** · `Lev 21:1–24:23` · priestly purity; the **festival calendar** (23).

> **32. Behar** · `Lev 25:1–26:2` · the Sabbatical year and the **Jubilee**. *(Often doubled with Bechukotai.)*

> **33. Bechukotai** · `Lev 26:3–27:34` · blessings and curses; vows.

# BAMIDBAR / Numbers — 10 parashot

> **34. Bamidbar** · `Num 1:1–4:20` · the census and the camp's arrangement. *(Read before Shavuot.)*

> **35. Naso** · `Num 4:21–7:89` · the Nazirite vow; the **Priestly Blessing** (6:24–26); the chieftains' offerings. *(The longest parashah.)*

> **36. Beha'alotcha** · `Num 8:1–12:16` · the menorah; the second Passover; the quail; Miriam's *tzaraat*.

> **37. Shelach** · `Num 13:1–15:41` · the **twelve spies** and the forty-year decree; the *tzitzit* (fringes) commandment.

> **38. Korach** · `Num 16:1–18:32` · **Korah's rebellion** and its swallowing; Aaron's budding staff.

> **39. Chukat** · `Num 19:1–22:1` · the red heifer; Moses strikes the rock (and forfeits the land); the bronze serpent; Miriam and Aaron die.

> **40. Balak** · `Num 22:2–25:9` · **Balaam and his donkey**; the unwilling blessing ("how goodly are your tents," *Mah Tovu*, in liturgy); Baal-Peor.

> **41. Pinchas** · `Num 25:10–30:1` · Phinehas's zeal; the second census; the **daughters of Zelophehad** (inheritance); the festival offerings.

> **42. Matot** · `Num 30:2–32:42` · vows; the war on Midian; the Transjordan tribes. *(Often doubled with Masei.)*

> **43. Masei** · `Num 33:1–36:13` · the wilderness itinerary; boundaries; cities of refuge.

# DEVARIM / Deuteronomy — 11 parashot

> **44. Devarim** · `Deut 1:1–3:22` · Moses' opening retrospective. *(Read before Tisha B'Av.)*

> **45. Va'etchanan** · `Deut 3:23–7:11` · the **Decalogue restated**; the **Shema** (6:4–9) — Judaism's central declaration. *(Shabbat Nachamu.)*

> **46. Eikev** · `Deut 7:12–11:25` · reward for obedience; "man does not live by bread alone"; the second Shema paragraph.

> **47. Re'eh** · `Deut 11:26–16:17` · blessing and curse; the central sanctuary; dietary law; tithes; the festivals.

> **48. Shoftim** · `Deut 16:18–21:9` · **judges and justice** ("justice, justice shall you pursue," 16:20); kings, prophets, and the laws of war.

> **49. Ki Teitzei** · `Deut 21:10–25:19` · a dense block of civil and family law (the most individual commandments of any parashah).

> **50. Ki Tavo** · `Deut 26:1–29:8` · firstfruits; the covenant blessings and the severe curses (*Tochacha*).

> **51. Nitzavim** · `Deut 29:9–30:20` · the covenant renewed; "**choose life**"; return (*teshuvah*) is always possible. *(Read before Rosh Hashanah; often doubled with Vayelech.)*

> **52. Vayelech** · `Deut 31:1–31:30` · Moses' final acts; the Torah written and entrusted.

> **53. Ha'azinu** · `Deut 32:1–32:52` · the **Song of Moses** — a poetic summary of the covenant.

> **54. V'Zot HaBerachah** · `Deut 33:1–34:12` · Moses blesses the tribes; Moses dies on Nebo. **Always read on Simchat Torah**, immediately followed by re-starting Bereshit — the cycle never closes.

## Festivals and the special readings (Megillot)
The **Five Scrolls (Hamesh Megillot)** of the Ketuvim are read at festivals,
linking the Writings to the calendar: **Song of Songs** (Passover), **Ruth**
(Shavuot), **Lamentations** (Tisha B'Av), **Ecclesiastes** (Sukkot), **Esther**
(Purim). See the corresponding `→R1` entries.
