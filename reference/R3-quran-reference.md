# R3 — The Quran: Sura Reference

The 114 *suras* (chapters) of the Quran — their names, order, Meccan/Medinan
classification, recitation divisions, and principal contents — with special
attention to the figures and narratives shared with the Bible (`R1`) and Torah
(`R2`) and *how the Quranic tellings differ*. Framed in its own right: as Muslims
receive it, the Quran is the direct, recited speech of God (*kalam Allah*) revealed
to the Prophet Muhammad through the angel Gabriel (*Jibril*) over c. 610–632 CE.

**Companion files:** `R0` rubric · `R1` Bible · `R2` Torah · `R4` Hadith ·
`R5` ANE sources. Cross-corpus links use `→R1`, `→R2`, `→R4`.

## How the Quran is structured

- **114 suras**, each made of *ayat* (verses; "signs"). All but Sura 9 open with the *Basmala* ("In the name of God, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful").
- **Order is not chronological.** After the short opening (Al-Fatiha), suras run roughly **longest to shortest**. The traditional order of revelation is different and is itself a field of study (*asbab al-nuzul*, "occasions of revelation").
- **Meccan vs. Medinan.** Suras revealed before the *Hijra* (the 622 CE migration to Medina) are **Meccan** — generally shorter, rhythmic, and focused on God's oneness (*tawhid*), resurrection and judgment, and the stories of earlier prophets. **Medinan** suras (post-Hijra) are generally longer and address law, community life, and relations with Jews, Christians, and opponents. Traditional markers: the address "O mankind" (Meccan) vs. "O you who believe" (Medinan). The conventional split is roughly **86 Meccan / 28 Medinan**, with about **a dozen disputed** (marked *‡* below).
- **Recitation divisions.** The text is also partitioned into **30 *juz'*** (and 60 *hizb*) for reading across a month — the basis of communal recitation, especially in Ramadan.
- **The shared prophets.** Many biblical figures appear as **prophets** (*anbiya'*) in a single line of *islam* (submission) culminating in Muhammad. The Quran assumes familiarity with their stories and retells them selectively, for moral and theological emphasis rather than continuous narrative — with notable differences from the biblical versions (detailed below).

## The shared prophets — and how the Quran differs from the Bible

The Quran retells biblical material to make theological points (God's oneness, the
unity of the prophetic message, judgment), not as continuous history. Recurring
differences from `R1`/`R2`: prophets are sinless models of *islam*; stories are
told in fragments across many suras; and several details diverge sharply.

- **Adam** (`2`, `7`, `15`, `20`) — God teaches Adam "the names"; the angels bow but **Iblis (Satan) refuses**, the origin of his fall. Adam and his wife err but **both repent and are forgiven** — there is no doctrine of inherited original sin (contrast `→R1` Gen 3 / Romans 5). **→R5:** the bowing-angels motif appears in earlier Jewish/Christian apocrypha.
- **The two sons of Adam** (`5:27–32`) — the accepted/rejected offerings and the murder, as in `→R1` Gen 4, but **the brothers are unnamed** (later tradition: Qabil and Habil); a **raven** scratches the earth to teach the killer to bury the body (not in Genesis); and **5:32** universalizes the saving/taking of one life as if all humanity — closely paralleling **Mishnah Sanhedrin 4:5** (`→R2`). One of the clearest Quran–rabbinic textual links.
- **Noah / Nuh** (`11`, `71`, and others) — the flood and the ark, but with extended **preaching** to a mocking people, and **one of Noah's sons refuses the ark and drowns** (11:42–46) — absent from Genesis. **→R5:** Gilgamesh/Atrahasis.
- **Abraham / Ibrahim** (`2`, `6`, `14`, `21`, `37`) — the **idol-smashing** youth (21:51–70; cf. the Jewish midrash, not the biblical text); the builder of the **Kaaba** with Ishmael (2:127); and the near-sacrifice (`37`), where the son is **unnamed in the text but generally identified as Ishmael** in later tradition (contrast Isaac in `→R1` Gen 22) — the basis of *Eid al-Adha*.
- **Lot / Lut** (`7`, `11`, `26`) — preaches against his people's sins before their destruction; a prophet in his own right.
- **Joseph / Yusuf** (`12`) — uniquely told **in full, in order**, and called "the best of stories." Differences from Genesis: God's providence and Joseph's **prophethood** are foregrounded; Jacob senses the brothers' treachery; the women of the city cut their hands in awe of Joseph's beauty (12:31); Potiphar's wife (named **Zulaykha** only in later tradition) publicly confesses. Revealed late in Mecca, traditionally to console the Prophet. **→R1:** Gen 37–50; **→R5:** the *Tale of Two Brothers*.
- **Moses / Musa** (`2`, `7`, `20`, `28`, `79`, and the most-mentioned prophet) — the birth and rescue, Midian, the burning bush, Pharaoh and the plagues, the sea, and the golden calf (made by **al-Samiri**, a figure not in the Bible). Sura `18` adds **Moses and al-Khidr**, a mysterious sage — a story with no biblical parallel.
- **David / Dawud** and **Solomon / Sulayman** (`21`, `27`, `34`, `38`) — David is given the **Psalms (Zabur)** and a beautiful voice the mountains echo; Solomon commands the **wind, jinn, and the speech of animals**, and tests the **Queen of Sheba (Bilqis)** — elaborations beyond `→R1` Kings.
- **Jonah / Yunus** (`10`, `37`, `68`) — the fish and the gourd; notably, **his people repent and are spared** (10:98), unlike the ambiguous ending of `→R1` Jonah.
- **Job / Ayyub** (`21`, `38`) — a brief model of **patient endurance**, without the long debate of `→R1` Job.
- **Mary / Maryam** (`3`, `19`) — the only woman named in the Quran; **Sura 19** narrates the **virgin conception** and Jesus speaking from the cradle. Mary is honored as among the greatest of women.
- **Jesus / Isa** (`3`, `4`, `5`, `19`, `43`) — a **prophet and Messiah, born of a virgin**, who performs miracles (giving life to clay birds, 3:49) — but **not divine and not the "son of God"** (a phrase the Quran emphatically rejects, e.g. 112), and the **crucifixion is denied** (4:157, "they did not kill him… but it was made to appear so to them"). He foretells a messenger named **Ahmad** (61:6). The sharpest theological divergence from `→R1`.
## Major theological themes (a thematic index)
- **Tawhid** (the absolute oneness of God): `1`, `112`, `2:255`, `59`.
- **Prophethood** (the unity of all messengers): `2`, `3`, `21`, `26`.
- **Resurrection & Judgment**: `75`, `78`–`84`, `99`–`101`.
- **Law & community** (Medinan): `2`, `4`, `5`, `24`, `33`, `65`.
- **Mercy & forgiveness**: `39`, `40`, `55`.
## Most-recited / most-emphasized in tradition
Al-Fatiha (`1`, in every prayer); Ayat al-Kursi (`2:255`); the Light Verse (`24:35`);
Ya-Sin (`36`, for the dying); Al-Mulk (`67`, nightly protection); Al-Ikhlas (`112`,
"a third of the Quran"); and the two "refuges," Al-Falaq and An-Nas (`113`–`114`).

## Apparatus (R3)
**Coverage:** all 114 suras with name, classification, opening juz', and a content
line; prose treatment of the shared-prophet narratives and their divergences from
`R1`/`R2`; thematic and recitation indices.
**On classification:** Meccan/Medinan follows the common (Egyptian-edition)
tradition; the dozen genuinely disputed suras are marked *‡*. Some suras contain
verses of the other type; only the dominant classification is given.
**On names/transliteration:** sura names and spellings vary across translations;
common forms are used.
**Not scored:** per project design, four-axis scores live in `R1`; shared figures
are cross-referenced rather than re-scored.
